2025-06-17
After a year of due diligence and operational exploration, Samaritan Health Services and Santiam Hospital and Clinics announced their mutual decision to cease pursuit of formal affiliation. The nonprofits stated they remain committed to their missions of service within the region and will continue to look for ways to collaborate that might benefit the health and well-being of the community from the mid-Willamette Valley through the central Oregon coast regions. Patient care, they said, should not be impacted by this decision.

The nonprofit, community-based health systems remain committed to their missions of service within the region and will continue to look for ways to collaborate that might benefit the health and well-being of the community from the mid-Willamette Valley through the central Oregon coast regions. 2025-05-23
Marty Cahill, the new CEO of Samaritan Health Services, addressed the public regarding the challenges facing healthcare in Oregon and Samaritan. He stated that healthcare in Oregon is in a tough spot, with many health systems operating at a loss due to rising costs and decreasing reimbursement from government payers. He emphasized that Samaritan is not immune to these pressures and is working to ensure its sustainability.
2025-05-16
Samaritan Health Services is considering closing maternity care services at its Lincoln City and Lebanon hospitals as a cost-cutting move. This is due to financial struggles facing Samaritan, like most other hospital chains in Oregon, including rising operating costs, capacity issues, and stagnant payments from insurance companies and government payers. No final decisions have been made, and Samaritan is evaluating these potential changes over the next few months.
2025-05-16
Crisis centers are set to open across the InterCommunity Health Network CCO service territory in Benton, Linn, and Lincoln counties to address the growing need for mental health services. Construction is nearly finished on the Benton County Crisis Center in Corvallis and the Linn County Crisis Center in Albany. The Lincoln County Crisis Center will be in Newport. These centers aim to provide quick access to crisis services without appointments, regardless of the ability to pay.
2025-04-15
Samaritan Health Services has named Marty Cahill, MBA, as the organization's next president and chief executive officer. Cahill has been with Samaritan Health Services since 2007 and most recently served as the chief operating officer of their hospitals. His appointment, effective May 19, 2025, follows the December 2024 resignation announcement of Doug Boysen. The leadership change comes at a time when rural systems like Samaritan are struggling with inflationary pressures and lagging public and private insurance reimbursements.
2025-03-22
Corvallis-based Samaritan Health Services has formally notified its bank that it likely violated its bond terms last year due to losses. While CEO Doug Boysen downplayed the notice, he said the finances in Oregon health care appear untenable statewide and it's the system's “duty” to seek growth while contemplating possibilities such as a merger or one or more outside partners. He stressed that no specific plan has been adopted, and no specific conversations with other health systems or companies have taken place. Samaritan also operates two insurance plans and is facing financial pressures similar to many rural and small regional health care systems.
2024-12-26
Samaritan Health Services President and Chief Executive Officer Doug Boysen announced plans to step down in 2025. Boysen has been working with the Samaritan Health Services board to develop a transition plan. He stated that he has given his all as a leader and feels it is the right time for new leadership at Samaritan.
