A U.S. GAO report highlights issues with the VA's Foreign Medical Program (FMP), including delays in reimbursing veterans and providers due to the continued use of paper checks instead of electronic payments, despite federal law. The program also has vacant staff positions hindering timely claims processing and has not comprehensively assessed fraud risks, despite evidence of potential fraud.
2025-02-19
A U.S. GAO report examined the VA Foreign Medical Program (FMP), noting that despite a federal law calling for electronic payments, the program still uses paper checks, leading to lost or delayed reimbursements. The report also found that many authorized staff positions to speed up the reimbursement process remain vacant. In fiscal year 2024, the program processed only 37 percent of claims within its goal of 45 days. The report recommends actions to improve the program's efficiency and timeliness.

State Leadership

Doug Collins

Secretary of Veterans Affairs

Confirmed by the United States Senate on February 4, 2025, and sworn in on February 5, 2025. Oversees the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs in its mission to provide health, education, disability, funerary, and financial benefits earned by Veterans of the United States Armed Forces.

Paul R. Lawrence

Deputy Secretary of Veterans Affairs

Nominated by President Donald J. Trump and confirmed by the United States Senate on March 27, 2025, and sworn in on March 28, 2025. Serves as the Department's second-in-command and Chief Operating Officer. Previously served as VA's 7th Under Secretary for Benefits from 2018 to 2021. Served as a Captain in the U.S. Army. Holds a Master of Arts and Ph.D. in Economics from Virginia Tech and a Bachelor of Arts in Economics from the University of Massachusetts, Amherst. Has 35 years of experience solving management problems in large, complex organizations and is the author of several books on effective and efficient government. Recognized with the 2019 Government Executive of the Year Award and selected three times as one of Federal Computer Week's top 100 public service leaders.

Dr. Shereef Elnahal

Under Secretary for Health

Appointed by President Joseph R. Biden and confirmed by the Senate on July 21, 2022. Leads the largest integrated health system in the nation, the Veterans Health Administration (VHA). Second tour of duty at VA, having served as VA's Assistant Deputy Under Secretary for Health for Quality, Safety, and Value from 2016 through 2018 and as a White House Fellow in the VA from 2015-16. Co-founded the VHA Innovation Ecosystem. Holds an M.D. from Harvard Medical School and an M.B.A. with Distinction from Harvard Business School.

Karen Brazell

Nominee for Under Secretary for Benefits

Nominated to head the Veterans Benefits Administration as of June 20, 2025. Served in the Army from 1984 to 1988. Worked as a defense contractor before becoming a civil servant with the Department of the Navy in 2006. Served as chief of staff for the White House military office from 2015 to 2018 and moved to the VA in 2018. Previously worked at the VA during the first Trump administration as an acting assistant secretary for the VA Office of Enterprise Integration and as the VA's chief acquisition officer. Served as a senior adviser to Department of Veterans Affairs Secretary Doug Collins since January.

Alan Boehme

Nominee for Assistant Secretary for Information and Technology

Nominated as of June 20, 2025. Industry chief technology officer who has worked at retailer H&M Group, Procter & Gamble and Coca-Cola.

Christopher D. Syrek

Chief of Staff

Listed as Chief of Staff in information regarding the Office of the Secretary.

Jen Burch

Deputy Chief of Staff

Appointed Deputy Chief of Staff at the Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) on March 3, 2025. Advises senior leadership, oversees policy implementation, and provides management oversight. Veteran and military spouse. Served two years as a Professional Staff Member for the U.S. House Committee on Veterans Affairs, Subcommittee on Health. Served as a Government Affairs Specialist at Wounded Warrior Project. Deployed to Kandahar, Afghanistan in 2010-2011 with the U.S. Air Force. Pursuing a Master of Public Health at George Washington University and holds a Bachelor of Arts in Global Studies from the University of Denver.

Brett Rusch

Executive Director

Assistant professor of psychiatry at Geisel School of Medicine. Joined the VA in 2015 as Chief of Mental Health and Behavioral Science Service and was appointed Chief of Staff in December 2016. Received a Bachelor's Degree in Psychology from the University of Wisconsin and completed medical school and a psychiatry residency at the University of Wisconsin School of Medicine and Public Health.

Daniel O'Rourke

Chief of Staff

Started his VA career at White River Junction in 1996 as a cardiologist and was named the Chief of Cardiology in 2004. Served as Acting Chief of Staff before being appointed permanently. Has extensive experience in clinical care, research and health care administration. Earned his MD at SUNY Upstate Medical University and completed internal medicine training at the University of Pittsburgh and cardiology fellowship at Dartmouth-Hitchcock Medical Center. Holds a master's degree in healthcare research from The Dartmouth Institute and a master's degree in education from Xavier University. Board certified in internal medicine, cardiovascular disease and nuclear cardiology. Associate Professor of Medicine at the Geisel School of Medicine at Dartmouth.

Becky (Daley) Rhoads

Associate Director

Began her VA career at the White River Junction VA Medical Center in June, 2009 as a staff audiologist and quickly selected for the Chief of Audiology position. Moved to health care administration in 2016 and has served Veterans through the Executive Assistant to the Director role since that time. A 2017 graduate of the VA Health Care Leadership Development Program (HCLDP). Graduate of the University of Vermont with a dual degree in Communication Sciences and Psychology. Earned a Doctorate in Audiology from Northeastern University in 2008.
