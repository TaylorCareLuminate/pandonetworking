Unipile Processes

Processes that run each day through Unipile, using a BDR's own LinkedIn account. Each kind of process is set up, scheduled and reviewed here, side by side. Everything that goes out on LinkedIn is sent by the LinkedIn engine, so its limits always apply (for example, never more than 20 comments a day per account), along with its do-not-contact list and its pauses.

LinkedIn accounts today

What each BDR's Unipile account has done so far today (by the engine's day). All processes for the same account share these limits.

For the Post comments process: it drafts a short comment in the BDR's voice on recent posts by their target contacts, and grades the comment and the post. Good drafts wait here for an admin, then (depending on the BDR's setting) for the BDR. Very interesting posts are also saved as repost ideas and feed the BDR's post ideas.

What it does

LinkedIn has no notification feed a program can read, but it does show these on My Network → Catch Up (Birthdays, Job changes, Education). Each run reads Catch Up for this BDR's account to see who to look at, then reads that person's profile to confirm they are a 1st-degree connection and to get the facts. Only people on this BDR's list (My Leads) are used, matched by their profile link, never by name. On a birthday it sends a short greeting you chose below (month and day only are kept, never the year). On a new job or role it sends a congratulations and updates their title and company in our records to match LinkedIn. Education updates are written down under Birthdays & new jobs; no message is sent for them yet. Never someone on the do-not-contact list, and never more than one greeting to a person in a day. Every message is one of the fixed wordings below; nothing is written by AI. An admin sets up the Catch Up requests once under Settings (“Catch Up source”). Profiles are also read on a schedule, as a backup, unless you choose Catch Up only.

Catch Up is much lighter on LinkedIn than reading every profile. If it is not set up yet, "Catch Up, then profiles" simply reads profiles.

This watches the comments the LinkedIn engine posted for this BDR (on a job seeker's post, a contact's post, anywhere the engine commented). Each one is read again on a schedule, often while it is new and less often as it ages, and anything new is recorded under Comment activity: replies (what they wrote, who, and whether it was the author of the post) and likes (how many, and who when LinkedIn says). What it does

Looks at the connection requests other people have sent to this BDR on LinkedIn that are still waiting, and accepts them: all of them, or only the ones the AI picks using the rules you write below. The AI reads the sender's headline and the note they attached; when that is not enough to tell, it reads their profile (within the limit below). Nobody is ever declined. A request that is not accepted simply stays waiting on LinkedIn, and each one is listed in the run with the reason, so a person can still accept it there. People on the do-not-contact list are never accepted, even in “all” mode. Nothing is sent to anyone: accepting is the only thing this does. It follows the LinkedIn engine's switches: while the engine is off or in dry run (and while “Preview only” is ticked below) it accepts nothing and only records what it would accept.

“All of them” also accepts spam and fake profiles. “Only the ones the AI picks” is a quick, low-cost AI call for each new request.

 Catch Up and notifications source

            
Unipile has no Catch Up or notifications call, but it can make any LinkedIn data request for a connected account. The request that loads the Catch Up cards (birthdays, job changes, education), and the one that loads the notifications page, each have to be copied once from Chrome. They are shared by every BDR, and read only: cookies and tokens are never kept (Unipile uses the connected account's own login).

            
For notifications: do the same steps on the notifications page (reload it with Fetch/XHR open, find the request whose response lists the notifications you can see, Copy as cURL), and choose LinkedIn notifications below. The Test shows each notification found, one row each.
