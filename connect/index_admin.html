Selling
Pitch & Demo Walkthrough How to describe what we do and walk a prospect through the demo pages. 8 steps
Pages: Scheduled Meetings (Demo) Message Outcomes Success Analysis
  1. Open the Demos index
    The demos pages are open to everyone. Open the Demos index, which links to Scheduled Meetings, Outcomes, Success Analysis and the simple Outcomes Calculator. Open them before the call — they take a little while to load.
  2. Describe us the right way
    We are not a BDR company. Say: “We offer executive networking services for executives who are too busy to do their own LinkedIn networking — but who live and die by it.” A lot of what we do is on LinkedIn, constantly networking for them into prospects, partners and investment partners.
  3. Give the value proposition
    Expanding the network of your senior leaders — who can open doors nobody else can — is the best and lowest-cost way into premier accounts. We do this for multiple CEOs and presidents, at very high quality, with white-glove / concierge service.
  4. Tell the founder story
    Our founder was president of an organization built on research and networking into healthcare leaders. When he left, he was surprised how unintuitive this was elsewhere. The wrong turn almost everyone makes is sending messages about themselves, their company and how good they are. What works is listening — understanding and engaging other people.
  5. Show real messages: Scheduled Meetings
    Show examples so they can see these are executive-to-executive messages: thoughtful, well written, and not “AI-ish” or salesy.
  6. Show results: Message Outcomes
    Walk through the overall trends — meetings scheduled and replies. Then explain the strategies: Post replies (we reply to one of their posts); Common ground (we compare the executive whose account we run with the person we reach out to and find overlap); Organization news (the fallback when there is no interesting recent post and no common ground — we mention org news or their profile).
  7. Show the science: Success Analysis
    Tell them our founder is a statistician, so everything is statistically based and we use a random forest model. Scroll to Deep Dive Research and open the sections: opening hook, tone, questions added, opener style, message type. Every message that goes out is graded by AI in about 20 different ways, so we know what is and is not working.
  8. Move to a meeting and proposal
    When they say they are interested, set the meeting and follow with a proposal the same day — see Build a Proposal.
Build a Proposal (Outcomes Calculator) Tailor a personalized proposal PDF the same day as the meeting (about 20 minutes). 10 steps
Pages: Outcomes Calculator
  1. Open the right calculator
    Use Outcomes Calculator for proposals. (The one in the open demos section is a simpler, limited version — do not use it for proposals.)
  2. Pick the pipeline type
    Ongoing pipeline — an almost unlimited audience (thousands and thousands of people), so we never run out. Finite pipeline — a defined audience, e.g. a total addressable market of 3,000 people. Do not propose months of outreach when the reality is only about 3½ months of runway.
  3. Fill in the basics
    Prospect name; scenario (usually “Management of 1 LinkedIn account”); your name and title; who we are reaching out to (e.g. IT executives at health systems, cancer clinics) — this shows we understand their prospect base. Add their website and upload their logo.
  4. Introduction letter
    Click Tailor letter with AI. The AI-written generic examples are no longer used — we do not do generic examples anymore.
  5. Generate from Connect analysis
    Pick from the markets we have estimates for (payers, physician groups, health systems, etc.). They are listed from most engaging / highest response down to least. Set the number of LinkedIn accounts and click Generate. Expected message volumes appear below.
  6. Review costs
    Check the cost section. You can round the total, e.g. $4,234 → $4,200.
  7. Risk-based scheduling
    We bill the first month, do not bill a second month, and keep going until we reach the number of meetings they expect. If we cannot reach it, we refund — that is how it is laid out legally. We charge up front because we want to see commitment, and we only want committed clients. In practice we keep going until we get the meetings.
  8. Build the live example
    Enter the BDR leader from their company who will do outreach in their name, plus company and website. For “what do we do” write the hook we use in campaigns: 80–150 characters, clear, not salesy or pushy, and something that makes the reader think “oh, interesting.” Press the button to build the leader profile.
  9. Add sample profiles
    In ChatGPT ask for 5 profiles and LinkedIn URLs of senior executives at the target (e.g. healthcare payers such as Aetna, Blue Cross Blue Shield). Check they look right, paste them in, and generate messages. Currently the generated messages are not great — edit them yourself for now (fix is coming).
  10. Export and send
    Export to PDF. A really nice, personalized proposal takes about 20 minutes, so it can go out the same day. Once they are ready to sign, Joe helps with the contract and BoldSign — then start Onboard a New Client & BDR.
Onboarding
Onboard a New Client & BDR HeyReach workspace, customer + BDR enrollment, LinkedIn sign-in with a 2FA key, audit & fix, and About Me. 11 steps
Pages: LinkedIn Account Setup About Me
  1. Create the HeyReach workspace
    Make sure the company exists in HeyReach. For a new customer, create a new workspace there.
  2. Generate the HeyReach API key
    In the new workspace click the puzzle piece (Integrations) on the left → HeyReach API → generate a new API key and copy it.
  3. Add the customer in Admin Email Controls
    Open Pando Admin → Email Controls and add the customer: full name, domain, email of the key person we work with, and the API key you just copied. Then click Enable HeyBridge LinkedIn automation for this customer.
  4. Easy BDR Enrollment
    On the same page go to Quick Actions → Easy BDR Enrollment. Select the customer, enter the person’s name, email and LinkedIn URL, and click Find HeyReach account. It will say it cannot find one — that is fine: choose “None of these / no HeyReach account yet” and click Enroll BDR. Everything is set up but not fully finished; you come back to finish it below. They now show up in our records.
  5. Get their LinkedIn two-factor key
    Open LinkedIn Account Setup and walk the person through it. On LinkedIn: Me → Settings & Privacy → Sign in & security → Two-step verification. If it is already on, turn it off and back on. They will see a QR code; beneath it is a long key (GUID-like code) — ask them to send it to us.
  6. Finish their authenticator
    They then scan the QR code with their authenticator app (open the app, tap the camera / QR button, scan). If LinkedIn was already in their authenticator, they must delete it first. Once we have the key we never need a six-digit code from them again.
  7. Create the invite link
    In LinkedIn Account Setup select the company from the list, select the BDR (already there from enrollment), enter the two-factor key, and click Create invite link. Copy the link and send it; they sign in with it. No more team-share or typing codes.
  8. Verify in HeyReach
    When they say they are signed in, check the HeyReach accounts and make sure you can see them.
  9. Audit & Fix
    Back on LinkedIn Account Setup select the company, scroll to BDR leaders, open it, find the person and click Audit & fix. This finishes the account creation (if everything is already fine, nothing changes).
  10. Set up About Me
    Open About Me , select the BDR, and click Generate AI suggestions — it looks at their profile and proposes statements. Click to add them, or interview the person and add I-statements yourself. This step can be done any time after they are in our system (even before HeyReach is finished).
  11. Connectors and team members
    Use the same process for connectors and team members — except those people also need an email address and an Outlook mailbox created.

Building a Campaign
Build a Hypothesis Group From a ChatGPT strategy session to a reviewed hypothesis group with organizations loaded. 8 steps
Pages: Research Hypothesis Documentation
  1. Run the Pando outreach strategy skill
    In ChatGPT open the company skills and load the Pando outreach strategy skill. Tell it who the customer is, who we are reaching out to, and their value proposition. Ask “why do you win?” — understanding why they win business tells you what actually works.
  2. Write the hooks
    The goal is to define hooks: 80–150 character summaries of what they do that are interesting to the people we contact. Too salesy turns people off; too bland gets no interest. Example: “We accelerate sales by extending the reach of your most influential executives — on LinkedIn, as a white-glove service they do not have to worry about.” Consider segmenting into several hypothesis groups for different outreach. ChatGPT produces a PDF report with examples and a Word hypothesis document.
  3. Import into a new group
    Open Research Hypothesis Documentation , pull up the customer, click Open new hypothesis group → Import from document, and paste the document. This is only a first pass — the group is not good until you review every tab.
  4. Overview tab
    The group name matters because it shows up in reporting. The hypothesis statement does not change outcomes, but keep it clear for reporting.
  5. Review instructions (who to contact)
    ChatGPT will build include / exclude instructions. About 90% of the time they should be about people and titles only — not types of organization, which blocks good contacts. The other ~10% is for customers who are very sensitive and need an extra careful check.
  6. Message review instructions
    The first four are defaults — keep them. Delete most of the rest, because what you want to say belongs in the Variations: a background statement (“here is what we do”) and a mention statement (“say this in the message”). You can add several variations.
  7. Load organizations
    Most of the time you go from an organization list to the people at those organizations. In ChatGPT ask for a list of organizations that fit and to research each organization’s LinkedIn URL (faster and more accurate than the system finding it). Then use the CSV upload on the Organizations tab.
  8. Or go straight to contacts
    Sometimes it is easier to skip organizations — e.g. “every high-income earner in the Dallas area.” Then use a profile search in Find Contacts Directly (Manage Data).
Run the Full Pipeline Scan organizations for contacts and generate messages, without paying twice for the same data. 9 steps
Pages: Full Pipeline
  1. Set up the run
    Open Full Pipeline . Load the client, the hypothesis group, select the BDR, and set how many messages to generate.
  2. Name the scan
    Scan names matter. Naming lets us run the same scan without re-scanning organizations — every scan costs money (Apify), and the same scan two months apart means paying double for the same data. Use names like “All executives” or “Senior leaders.”
  3. Choose who to find
    Select job functions and seniority levels (e.g. HR executives), how many contacts per organization, and locations. Locations are comma separated; just entering “United States” covers everything in the US.
  4. Be careful with job title keywords
    Use no more than about 10 words. Common words (and, has…) produce slop. If looking for dentists, use “dentist” and maybe “orthodontist” — only words likely in the profiles you want and unlikely in others. This searches across the whole profile, not only the title.
  5. Complex scans (optional)
    You can have AI classify organizations with a cheap model (e.g. Qwen) and apply different rules: “if it is a health system, scan by keywords; if it is a practice, take everyone senior.” That gives multiple groups in the same hypothesis group.
  6. Check the cost guard
    Defaults are fine. A common setting is about $10, which stops you scanning too wide. Scanning 10,000 people ($100) to make only 100 messages is $1/message — we cannot operate that way. Aim for 30–80% of scanned people being used. Not 100%, because you need a wide enough aperture to catch some you do not want (like swinging at some balls in baseball); but not so wide you get tons of them.
  7. Run and watch
    Click Run full pipeline. Messages are created and you can see how it is going. If it is not giving you what you want, stop it and adjust.
  8. Mop-up / strategy adjustment
    If you stop mid-search or results look off, export the organization report (section 3, organizations to scan). In ChatGPT open the Pando LinkedIn scan audit skill, upload the Excel, and ask: are we missing organizations? missing contacts? did we scan contacts we never created messages for?
  9. Scan again
    Add more organizations and edit the scan any time — every time you edit a scan, give it a new name so you do not repeat the same scan.
Find Contacts Directly (Manage Data) Workspaces, profile search with a grid strategy, Smart Cleanup, and finding missed contacts. 10 steps
Pages: Manage Data Research Hypothesis Documentation
  1. Understand workspaces
    Manage Data is the all-in-one data space. Contacts from Full Pipeline are saved to the BDR running it and maybe a workspace. A workspace stores organizations and contacts, is tied to a company, but not to a single BDR. You can merge, de-duplicate and move contacts between workspaces.
  2. Create a workspace
    To go straight to contacts (e.g. all executives in Dallas), click New workspace and tie it to the customer.
  3. Profile search
    Use Profile search (right side). Seniority levels, functions and departments work like Full Pipeline — unselect everything to get everything; select one or more to restrict. Also headcount, location (ask ChatGPT for a list, e.g. every US state or the cities in Northwest Indiana, one per line, and paste it), job titles (only a few distinctive words), and school.
  4. Know the 2,500 limit
    LinkedIn returns only 2,500 results per search. Repeating the same search returns the same 2,500 — and you pay each time. Instead run multiple non-overlapping searches (a grid): e.g. four regions. If a region has more than 2,500 people, split further (region × seniority level).
  5. Use query segmentation
    In Query segmentation click Locations: one search is run for every line you entered. Set max items to 2,500, start the search, and results are stored in the selected workspace (or a BDR — BDRs essentially have their own workspaces too).
  6. Clean up with Smart Cleanup
    Load a workspace’s contacts into Smart Cleanup and pick a model (models change; GPT-6 Luna is cheap, Qwen 3.8 Flash cheaper). Add an AI filter by organization, title and/or location (e.g. “keep only contacts in Texas”). It keeps only contacts that pass every filter you add.
  7. Deep profile review
    When AI must read the full profile (e.g. “only likely high-net-worth individuals”), load profiles, include experience and education, and write a detailed prompt that asks exactly what you want. Use a smarter model (GPT-6, Sonnet) — it costs a few to a few dozen dollars. You will learn which models fit which jobs.
  8. Protect your originals
    Sometimes create two workspaces and dump the keepers into the second so the original stays intact. You can also duplicate, move and adjust data on this page.
  9. Find missed contacts
    In Research Hypothesis Documentation open a company and press Locate to find all of its contacts (e.g. 5,000), then export. Give that export to ChatGPT with the history of outreach and ask which contacts were missed and for their LinkedIn URLs. This is helpful late in a campaign when you are scraping the bottom of the barrel.
  10. Tie contacts to a hypothesis group
    Before generating messages you can associate contacts with a hypothesis group using the batch hypothesis-group correlation, or just keep them in their own workspace and pick that as the source when you generate.
Messages
Generate Messages Full AI Generate for connection messages, plus harvest and fast modes. 9 steps
Pages: Generate Messages
  1. Choose the BDR and source
    Open Generate Messages — Full AI Generate is the most common. Select the BDR who will send. Then choose where connections come from: the BDR’s list, a workspace, other BDRs’ connections, contacts another BDR recently sent to, another BDR’s prospect pool, pasted LinkedIn URLs, or people you are already connected to.
  2. Load the hypothesis group
    Load and choose the hypothesis group. “Only contacts already associated” is more restrictive; usually pick any contacts because you already filtered them into a workspace. Running this associates them with the hypothesis group.
  3. Variations
    Choose a message variation, or leave the default smart variation tool, which constantly watches which variations work and favors them.
  4. Opus review
    The Opus review is the final clean-up pass. We learned multi-stage generation is required — otherwise messages do not turn out well. If you are confident your contact list is already clean, set exclude sensitivity to low or off.
  5. Prompts
    You can view and edit the prompts, and save them to the hypothesis group if you like.
  6. How many and in what order
    Set how many contacts to evaluate. “Only generate for contacts with more than 100 connections” is on by default. Prospects with more connections are most likely to reply — click to start with the most connections first; otherwise contacts are pulled randomly/alphabetically. Ignore the Reserve Queue for now.
  7. Extra research (optional)
    Add a search to feed the message, e.g. “What is the recent M&A activity of the organization?”
  8. Start
    Click Start Full AI Smart Generate.
  9. Other modes
    Harvest run — messages to people in a harvest pool (pools are defined on My Leads ; the default is people connected through our tool). Pick the person, usually exciting news / simple reactions, leave defaults — it creates follow-up messages. Fast prospect messages — the same message to many people; fast harvest — the same message to people you are already connected with. There is also a post search to find people by words in their posts.
Review & Edit Messages (Fast Connect Review) Batch-review a queue with ChatGPT, load the edits, and confirm messages reach HeyReach. 6 steps
Pages: Fast Batch Review Sent Messages & Results
  1. Open your queue
    Fast Batch Review is where all generated messages are stored. Select yourself. Connection requests and messages go through different channels, so pick the one you want — it loads what is in your queue.
  2. Export for review
    Use the Export button to get an Excel of the queue.
  3. Review with ChatGPT
    In ChatGPT open the LinkedIn message review skill and load the Excel. Ask it to check details and catch anything wonky or embarrassing — ask it to go slowly and tell it specific things to catch. It also adjusts your whole batch quickly, and does a nice job. It returns a JSON.
  4. Load the edits
    Copy the JSON and paste it into the JSON load box on the page. The messages are edited, then submit them.
  5. Confirm in HeyReach (new BDRs)
    Messages do not reach HeyReach immediately — they go every 30 minutes during the work day. For a brand-new BDR, open HeyReach, find the person, open Campaigns and confirm you see numbers.
  6. Edit messages already in progress
    To change messages already sent into the process (e.g. you realize every message should ask for a meeting up front), open Sent Messages , pull up all of that BDR’s messages, and edit the in-progress ones there.
Replies & Follow-ups
Handle Replies Work the LinkedIn Replies tab: categorize, tweak, send, and notify the BDR of meetings. 5 steps
  1. Open the LinkedIn Replies tab
    On this page, the LinkedIn Replies tab holds every reply that comes in. Replies outside our own outreach are filtered out. Each card shows a summary of the company.
  2. Always set a response category
    Choose the response category on every reply.
  3. Tweak the drafted reply and send
    Edit the suggested message almost every time — for declines the draft is often too long. Then press Send message.
  4. When someone agrees to meet
    Reply (e.g. “Looking forward to meeting with you”), then click Notify BDR. The email comes from you to the BDR — add the client’s assistant as a CC if useful, adjust the text, add a little about the person, and Send email. Then send the LinkedIn reply confirming you have it scheduled. Mark the contact as scheduled.
  5. Dismiss when done
    Work down the rest of the list and dismiss each reply once it is handled.
Weekly Follow-ups (Company Review Replies) Scan, draft and send follow-ups to everyone who replied — best done on Thursdays. 6 steps
Pages: Company Review Replies
  1. Understand the groups
    On Company Review Replies the counts along the left are: responded, responded — engaged, and responded — willing to meet. You are really deciding how many follow-ups to send. Our first response to someone is the first follow-up.
  2. Which group
    A simple “thanks” or thumbs-up (“would love to connect at some point”) is responded. Interest without saying they want to meet (“tell me about this”) is engaged. Willing to meet gets three follow-ups.
  3. Scan
    Run the scan for the group. It takes about a minute and updates the counts — some contacts move (e.g. 3 → 2) because a quick scan shows we have already replied.
  4. Draft follow-up sequences
    Click Draft follow-up sequences. It runs for about half an hour. Then open Follow-ups ready.
  5. Insert and tweak
    In each message a green section above shows the follow-up you can insert into the reply box; it pre-writes it for you. Tweak a lot: insert the calendar link or times; a very helpful line is “I’m so-and-so’s executive assistant reaching out — would love to connect with you.”
  6. Make it weekly
    Do this once a week — Thursdays are best — for every customer. The same page is also where you can see all replies that have come through for a customer.
Reporting & Routine
Client Review Meeting & Campaign Health Know the numbers before the call, and dig into struggling campaigns with analysis. 5 steps
Pages: Company Review Replies Message Outcomes Success Analysis Sent Messages & Results
  1. Pull up the customer
    For every client review pull up Company Review Replies and Message Outcomes for that customer. You must know your stuff: how many replies recently, how many people scheduled, and be able to go prospect by prospect (“how is this going for you?”). For a lot of replies, export all contacts and conversations and ask ChatGPT (e.g. “who in the last month mentioned X?”).
  2. Read the outcomes page
    On Outcomes see connections sent and accepted (and who), benchmarks against the global benchmark, each hypothesis group’s performance, and results by variation — the best way to report out.
  3. Dig into analysis
    Open Success Analysis . The data is re-run every Tuesday. Select the customer’s hypothesis groups. You can compare titles (VPs do better than CEOs), first-name use, and tone — e.g. a genuine tone gets a connection about 13% of the time, while salesy, pushy, generic or flattering tones get lower rates and no meetings.
  4. Diagnose a struggling group
    Open the group in question to see more: message length (are short messages hurting?), words that work and words that do not, and examples. Test different approaches.
  5. Adjust the campaign
    Use what you learn to edit the campaign. To change messages already in progress, go to Sent Messages and edit them there.
Daily & Weekly Routine The checklist each day, with extra items on Wednesdays and Thursdays. 7 steps
Pages: Fast Batch Review Generate Messages
  1. Check last night’s replies
    Open the LinkedIn Replies tab and handle the messages from the night before — see Handle Replies.
  2. Push live yesterday’s messages
    Push live any messages that were generated yesterday — see Review & Edit Messages (Fast Batch Review ).
  3. Refill accounts under 100 messages
    Open the BDR Dashboard tab and scan for accounts with fewer than 100 messages. For each one, run Full Pipeline or Generate Messages to get more.
  4. End of day: proposals
    Build and send any proposals from the day’s sales meetings — see Build a Proposal.
  5. Wednesdays
    Review any campaigns that are not performing well and make adjustments — see Client Review Meeting & Campaign Health.
  6. Thursdays
    Do the weekly follow-ups — see Weekly Follow-ups.
  7. Use the To-Do List tab
    The My To-Do List tab pops up the daily routine, with Wednesday and Thursday items added automatically.
