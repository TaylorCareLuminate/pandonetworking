Interview Script & Guidelines

Introductions:

Thanks for joining today. This role is focused on making outbound calls on behalf of our clients to schedule meetings. Most calls go to voicemail, with only a few live connections, so we're looking for people who can stay upbeat, professional, and persistent. I'll ask you some questions, and we'll do a couple of short roleplays to see how you handle calls. Sound good?

1. Phone Energy & Peppiness (20%)

Prompt: "Imagine you're calling me as a new business prospect. Give me a quick 15-second introduction." 👉 Listen for: upbeat tone, energy, warmth (not monotone).

2. Professionalism & Polished Tone (20%)

Prompt: "Now, imagine you're leaving a voicemail for a VP at a large company. Here's the script — read it to me as if you were actually leaving that voicemail." 👉 Listen for: clarity, pace, professionalism, confidence. VOICEMAIL SCRIPT (to share with candidate): "Hi Jon, this is [Your Name] calling on behalf of ABC Company. I noticed you're the head of digital engagement at XYZ company and wanted to reach out. ABC company partners with companies like yours to help global teams simplify digital engagement and deliver more consistent customer experiences across regions. I'd love to set up a brief 15-minute call with our digital strategy team to share what we're seeing across other global enterprises, and to hear your perspective. You can reach me at [Make up a number]. Again, that's [callback number]. Thank you, Jon, we look forward to connecting with you."

ROLE PLAY CALL SCRIPT (To share with candidate):

Prospect Background Name: Melissa Jordan Role: VP of Global Customer Experience & Digital Strategy Company: BrightWave Medical (a fictional mid-sized healthcare technology company) Background: Recently spoke at "HealthTech Engage 2024" on patient engagement and digital transformation. Script for Candidate (to use as intro) "Hi Melissa, this is [Your Name], I'm reaching out on behalf of ABC Company. I came across your role as VP of Global Customer Experience and Digital Strategy at BrightWave Medical — and your recent talk at HealthTech Engage on digital patient engagement really stood out. It seems like you're focused on scaling digital touchpoints while keeping the human side of healthcare." (Wait for reply) "[Customer A] has been helping similar healthcare technology companies — making digital engagement smoother for both patients and providers, while taking the load off internal teams. I'd love to set up a quick 15-minute conversation between you and our strategy team to exchange ideas and share what we're seeing across the industry. Would early next week — maybe Wednesday morning or Thursday afternoon — work better for you?"

3. Objection Handling (15%)

(Customer Objection – Choose 1 or 2 – DO NOT SHARE WITH CANDIDATE) • I'm not interested • We already have a solution for that • Can you just email me? 👉 Listen for: calm, polite redirection toward scheduling. *After the interview, please rank objection handling using the Objection handling reference at the end of this packet*

4. Appointment Closing (15%)

Roleplay continuation: After they pitch, you say: "Maybe, send me info." 👉 Look for: Do they close with a clear ask for a time? Example: "Absolutely, I'll send that over. While I have you, would Tuesday at 2pm or Thursday at 10am work better for a quick 15-minute call?"

5. Adaptability & Script Comfort (10%)

Prompt: "Sometimes our scripts aren't perfect. I'm going to give you a rough, AI-generated script. Try reading it in a way that sounds natural and conversational." Rough Script (intentionally) to share with Candidate: "Hello, this is [Your Name] and I am calling in representation of [Customer A], and the reason why this call is because we have been analyzing the industry and found numerous digital transformation things that companies are being challenged with, and we are believing that Philips, as a global digital innovation leader, might be interested to hear a conversation about how our solutions are able to help solve engagement strategy challenges in multiple regions. So, I am asking if you would like to accept to schedule a meeting with our senior leader to talk more details?" 👉 Look for: Do they adjust wording on the fly, or read it awkwardly word-for-word?

6. Reliability & Work-from-Home Setup (10%)

Questions: • "What's your home office setup like — do you have a quiet space, reliable internet, and a headset?" • "How many calls do you think you could comfortably make in an hour?" 👉 Look for: readiness to handle 20+ calls/hour, no major environment issues.

7. Experience & Motivation (10%)

Questions: • "Have you done outbound calling or appointment setting before?" • "This role is repetitive, with lots of voicemails. How do you keep yourself motivated?" • "How do you feel about a pay-per-call and pay-per-appointment structure?" 👉 Look for: persistence, comfort with performance-based pay, no fear of repetition.

Objection Handling Reference (For Interviewer Only)

Note: In a real-life situation, a really good employee can use empathetic reasoning (i.e. the ability to read the room) to determine the best course of action they should take. These responses are just examples of what we want to see in the response in a controlled interview setting. If the candidate responded differently than what you see here, but you feel that the response is great, make a note of what they said and how they responded (we may be able to use that in future training) and mark them accordingly.
Objection 1: "I'm not interested."

Good: "Okay, I understand. Thanks for your time." (Polite, but ends the call without pushing toward the goal.)

Better: "I hear you. A lot of leaders I speak with felt the same at first, but once they saw how [Customer A] helps companies reduce friction in digital engagement, they found it useful. Would you be open to a quick 15-minute call just to see if it's relevant?" (Acknowledges objection, offers quick value, gently asks again.)

Best: "I completely understand. Most people I call aren't actively looking when I first reach out. That's exactly why we keep it short; just a 15-minute conversation to see if it's even a fit. If not, no problem. Would next Tuesday or Thursday be better for you?" (Empathetic, reframes as low commitment, immediately offers time slots.)

Objection 2: "We already have a solution for that."

Good: "Okay, that makes sense. Thanks for letting me know." (Respectful but doesn't try to continue the conversation.)

Better: "That's great. Out of curiosity, what are you using today?" (pause) "I ask because a lot of our clients already had solutions in place, but have still found [Customer A] helped them in areas like scalability and ease of integration." (Acknowledges, asks a probing question, positions Customer as complementary.)

Best: "Perfect, that's exactly the kind of team we work with. Most of our clients already had something in place, they just wanted to improve adoption across regions or reduce complexity. It may or may not be relevant for you, but worth a short 15-minute call to compare notes. Would early next week work?" (Positions the objection as normal, reframes it as a reason to meet, and asks for time directly.)

Objection 3: "Just send me an email."

Good: "Sure, I can send you an email." (Accepts, but loses momentum.)

Better: "Absolutely, I'll send something over. Just so I send the most useful info — would it make sense to schedule a short 15-minute call where we can answer your questions?" (Agrees, but tries to reopen the scheduling door.)

Best: "Of course, I'll send you a quick summary. While I have you, most people find it easier to just set up a short call so they can ask questions directly instead of going back-and-forth over email. Would Tuesday or Thursday afternoon be better for a 15-minute call?" (Uses the email as a stepping stone, but still drives to a specific appointment.)
