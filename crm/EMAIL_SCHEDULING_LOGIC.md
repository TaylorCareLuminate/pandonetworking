# Email Scheduling Logic (First-time vs Follow-up)

This document explains how scheduling and follow-up behavior works across the CRM frontend and the Railway backend.

## Goals
- Be aware of first-time emails and follow-ups - schedule togetherSpecializations or areas of expertise
Availability and workload capacity
- Use the calendar of slots to schedule emails and LinkedIn. 
- Schedule emails iteratively, starts for outreach sets later once the max of 2 emails per domain per day fill up. 
- Schedule the whole set at a time so that follow up emails are scheduled the correct number of days after the prior email. After every one is scheduled, review domains to make sure that not more than 2 emails are sent to a domain on a specific day. 
- Ensure follow-ups always thread correctly and include the original email content

## Frontend (email_queue.html / mail_campaign_hotsheet.html)
1. Discovery
   - Loads emails (scheduled/sent) and calendar availability and email slots from `clemail` Firestore.
2. Classification
   - Splits pending emails into first-time vs follow-up using `type`, `followUpNumber`, and subject patterns.
3. Calendar of possible days and slots
   - Uses `email_calendar` settings (available/unavailable days) and `email_calendar_slots/slots` document which contains `slotsByDate` (map of `YYYY-MM-DD` -> array of ISO timestamps) generated by `email_calendar.html`.
   - UI shows a preview of the next 30 days of computed slots.
4. Slotting (slot-based)
   - First-time emails: fill days first, choosing the first free slot (>= 1 hour from now) on each eligible day, respecting max 2/domain/day and daily capacity. Slots already enforce spacing within the day.
   - Follow-ups: scheduled only after all first-time emails; earliest date is 5 days after the last email to that recipient, ensuring no more than two emails sent to a domain per day. 
5. Follow-up hygiene
   - Sets `type: 'follow_up'`, `replyType: 'reply'`, and `followUpToEmailId` (when last email id is known).
   - Ensures subjects start with `Re: ` and mirrors the original subject when available.
6. Persistence
   - Updates the backend via `PUT /emails/:id` with `sendAt`, `subject`, `type`, `replyType`, and `followUpToEmailId`.
   - Calendar page persists both day availability and computed slots to Firestore: `email_calendar/settings` and `email_calendar_slots/slots`.

## Backend (Railway/server.js)
- POST `/schedule-email`
  - If `followUpToEmailId` is provided:
    - Validates the original exists and was sent
    - Enforces minimum 5-day spacing vs original sent time
    - Prefixes subject with `Re: ` (if missing)
    - Adds proper threading headers (`In-Reply-To`, `References`)
    - Appends a formatted reply chain (HTML and text)
    - Stores `type: 'follow_up'` and preserves `replyType` (e.g., `reply`, `reply_all`)
- PUT `/emails/:id`
  - Accepts updates for `sendAt`, `subject`, `type`, `replyType`, `followUpToEmailId` for resorting operations
- Cron sender
  - Re-validates follow-ups before send: if too soon, defers to the earliest allowed time
  - Ensures `Re: ` subject and reply chain presence at send-time as a safety net
  - Treats provided `sendAt` as an exact slot time produced by the calendar; does not randomize times further

## Operational Notes
- Calendar availability and daily capacity control the schedule window and spacing.
- The frontend avoids weekend/holiday scheduling when calendar forbids it.
- The backend is authoritative for follow-up safety and ensures threading even if a legacy record bypassed the new client logic.

## Troubleshooting
- Follow-ups sending too soon: Backend now defers to 5+ days from original send.
- Missing reply chain: Schedule or send-time hygiene guarantees the original is included.
- No `Re:` on follow-ups: Both sides enforce the prefix; the backend is the last line of defense.
