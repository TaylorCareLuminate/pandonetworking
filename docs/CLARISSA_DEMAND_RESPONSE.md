## 4. Specific risk flags for counsel

- **Misclassification (her strongest point):** she lists control factors (company-provided leads, mandated software, set pay rates). This turns on the legal test, not our characterization. Counsel should decide how (and whether) to address it — the draft deliberately leaves it blank. Do not concede; do not litigate it in a letter.
- **Utah Code § 34-28-5 / 24-hour clock:** applies to *employees* on separation. If she is reclassified, final-pay timing and penalties could attach. Paying all genuinely-earned wages immediately is protective.
- **Retaliation:** terminating right after a wage complaint/legal threat is high-risk. Counsel should decide whether to separate the termination from the wage response, and document a legitimate, pre-existing, non-retaliatory basis. Do not reference her complaint as a reason.
- **No admissions:** avoid the words "error reduced your pay," "we owe you for those," "fraud," "fabricated." Use "the dashboard display overstated a total; pay was always based on calls placed to valid numbers."
- **Tone:** kind, factual, firm. No statutory back-and-forth in our letter — that's counsel's lane.
