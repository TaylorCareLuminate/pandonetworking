Jun 13, 2025
Jackson-based University of Mississippi Medical Center (UMMC) announced the completion of its acquisition of Merit Health Madison, a 67-bed facility located in Canton, Mississippi. Following the acquisition from a subsidiary of Community Health Systems, Merit Health Madison will now operate as UMMC Madison. This move builds upon an existing business relationship that began in 2019, where UMMC physicians were already performing procedures at the facility. The acquisition effectively expands UMMC's adult hospital capacity and operating room availability, providing another location for training future healthcare providers and increasing patient access to specialty services in the central Mississippi area. Merit Health Madison was noted as a well-maintained facility, and UMMC physicians are expected to utilize its operating rooms for select short-stay surgical procedures, which will allow the main University Hospital in Jackson to schedule more high-acuity and specialty cases. Merit Health leadership expressed excitement about the collaboration with UMMC to bring more healthcare resources to the region, highlighting the Canton facility's surgical capacity and the staff's readiness to work with UMMC surgeons to expand access to care for patients from Madison County and surrounding areas. Apr 25, 2025
Governor Tate Reeves vetoed a legislative bill aimed at reforming the certificate of need (CON) process, specifically citing ongoing litigation over a Jackson psychiatric hospital. The veto message argued that the bill unlawfully attempted to circumvent a court case by granting a CON to a single psychiatric hospital, Oceans Behavioral Hospital Jackson. A Senate amendment to the bill would have granted a new CON to Oceans and aimed to resolve a legal dispute with Merit Health Central concerning the amount of charity care Oceans must provide. Merit Health had sued Oceans in March of the previous year (2024), alleging that Oceans violated the law by using a workaround to avoid a State Department of Health requirement for providing more free or low-cost care than outlined in its CON application. Merit's lawsuit contended that Oceans not providing sufficient charity care would negatively impact Merit by directing more low-income patients to their facility. The Governor's veto message criticized the amendment as potentially showing favoritism and prejudice towards other market participants. Merit's lawsuit sought a ruling that the State Department of Health lacked the authority to treat Oceans' reopening as a change of ownership and that Oceans should undergo the full CON approval process.
