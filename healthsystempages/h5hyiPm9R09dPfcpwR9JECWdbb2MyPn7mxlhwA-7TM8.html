Recent News

Jun 16, 2025
A study conducted by Cleveland Clinic researchers highlights the critical importance of the timing of antibiotic doses in preventing the development of drug-resistant infections. The findings, published in Science Advances, utilized an evolutionary modeling approach called a “fitness seascape” to simulate how bacterial resistance emerges based on real-world treatment patterns. The study revealed that missing or delaying early antibiotic doses has a more significant impact on resistance than previously understood. This research could lead to more personalized prescribing practices and public health campaigns emphasizing the importance of adhering to antibiotic timing instructions from the outset of treatment. Scientists are also exploring antibiotic adjuvants and bacteriophage therapy as potential tools in combating resistant strains. [4, 16]
Jun 16, 2025
Dr. Joseph Khabbaza, a pulmonologist from Cleveland Clinic, participated in a clinical forum centered on updates in respiratory care, discussing biologic decision-making in asthma and COPD. The discussion focused on the growing need for clinicians to reevaluate traditional treatment pathways due to new insights into inflammatory endotypes and the blurring lines between asthma and COPD. Advancements in biologic therapies, such as dupilumab, benralizumab, and tezepelumab, were highlighted as fundamentally altering long-term disease control for previously difficult-to-treat patients. The approval of tezepelumab and dupilumab for severe asthma and eosinophilic COPD, respectively, provides broader options for tailored treatment and strengthens collaboration between allergists and pulmonologists for early patient identification. The latest episode of Cleveland Clinic's Neuro Pathways podcast for medical professionals features Megan Jack, MD, PhD, and Dennis Kao, MD, discussing brachial plexus injuries. The podcast explores the causes, diagnosis, and treatment of these nerve injuries, which often result from trauma. Dr. Jack and Dr. Kao, both part of Cleveland Clinic's multidisciplinary brachial plexus injury program, provide expert insight into nerve reconstruction and recent advancements in care, emphasizing the importance of early referral and a multidisciplinary approach. They also touched on diagnostic tools like EMG, ultrasound, and electrical stimulation, as well as surgical options. [34]
