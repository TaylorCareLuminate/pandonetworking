Recent News

Jul 08, 2025
Hilo Benioff Medical Center, part of the Hawaii Health Systems Corporation, has issued a Request for Proposals (RFP) for a parking lot expansion and covered drop-off project at MOB 1 & MOB 2. The solicitation number is 25-0437, and the deadline for proposals is July 8, 2025, at 2:00 PM HST. This project indicates planned facility expansion and infrastructure improvement at the hospital, which serves as the flagship hospital within the East Hawaii Region of HHSC.
Jun 03, 2025
In a significant move to enhance healthcare services in West Hawai'i, the Hawaii Health Systems Corporation West Hawaii Region (WHR), The Queen's Health Systems (Queen's), and Ali'i Health Center have entered into a shared workforce agreement. This landmark collaboration, formalized after approximately 18 months of discussions, unites Kona Community Hospital, Kohala Hospital, and provider groups from Queen's and Ali'i Health Center. The primary goal of this initiative is to maintain healthcare services locally and strengthen specialty care options available on the island. The agreement facilitates healthcare providers from all three participating organizations to work across each other's facilities. This is intended to create a more seamless and patient-centered care experience while improving access to specialized medical care closer to where patients live. According to Dr. Jeffrey Palmgren, Chief Medical Officer of WHR, this agreement represents a substantial advancement in collaborative efforts to address the healthcare needs of West Hawai'i communities. The shared workforce model aims to reduce the necessity for patients to travel off-island for referrals, expand the availability of a wider range of specialty care services, cultivate a unified physician group culture to aid in recruitment and retention of medical professionals, and establish a pipeline for future healthcare providers through Graduate Medical Education.
Jun 03, 2025
Kona Community Hospital (KCH), part of the Hawaii Health Systems Corporation's West Hawaii Region, celebrated the completion of its newly expanded pharmacy with a special blessing ceremony attended by Governor Josh Green, hospital officials, and state legislators on Monday, June 2, 2025. The long-awaited expansion, which broke ground nearly two years prior, includes a new compounding suite enabling KCH to safely prepare medications, including chemotherapy drugs, on-site. This enhancement is a significant step in improving healthcare access for West Hawai'i residents by eliminating the need to outsource this critical service, thus bringing important efficiencies and better serving patients. The $2.3 million project was funded by the Hawai'i State Legislature and Governor Green's administration. Hospital officials stated that this upgraded pharmacy is a milestone in healthcare access for the region, allowing many patients who previously had to travel to Hilo or Oahu for treatment to receive care closer to home. The new facility also supports longer beyond-use dates, improving efficiency and reducing costs, while strengthening emergency preparedness and enhancing the hospital's capacity for oncology and other specialized treatments. The new compounding suite is expected to be fully operational by the end of the summer.
May 07, 2025
Hawaii has joined a coalition of 18 other states and the District of Columbia in filing a lawsuit against the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) and its Secretary, Robert F. Kennedy Jr. The lawsuit challenges the abrupt termination of nearly $12 billion in critical public health grants to the states, arguing that these cuts exceed presidential authority and are unconstitutional. The states claim that these funding cuts are already causing harm, including the closure of labs monitoring the current measles outbreak and the cessation of other vital programs. Hawaii stands to lose over $89 million as a result of these cancellations, which fund essential services like data infrastructure, community support, substance abuse prevention, public health staffing, and upgrades for state labs. The coalition is seeking a temporary restraining order to invalidate the grant terminations and prevent HHS from reinstating them.
May 05, 2025
Potential cuts to the Medicaid program could have a significant impact on healthcare providers in Hawaii, including Hawaii Health Systems Corporation. According to an article, hospitals in Hawaii are already operating on very narrow margins, and rising labor costs further exacerbate financial pressures. Medicaid, known as Med-QUEST in Hawaii, is a crucial payer, covering over 400,000 residents, including a substantial portion of children and nursing home residents. While no immediate changes to Hawaii's Medicaid funding have been announced, discussions at the federal level about potential cuts, such as imposing work requirements or scaling back the federal share of costs, could force states like Hawaii to make difficult decisions to offset reductions. This broader challenge within the Medicaid system is relevant to HHSC as a significant provider of healthcare services in the state, particularly to vulnerable populations who rely on Medicaid for coverage. The state Legislature may need to address potential federal cuts in a future session.

Joyce M. Tamori

Vice President and Chief Financial Officer

Joyce M. Tamori is the Vice President and Chief Financial Officer at Hawaii Health Systems Corporation (HHSC), a position she has held since 2023. She has extensive experience in the healthcare industry. Prior to her role at HHSC, she was the VP, Special Projects, MHS at Kaiser Permanente from September 2022 to June 2023 and VP, CFO - MHS at Maui Health from March 2017 to September 2022. Tamori also held various positions at Hale Makua Health Services, including Chief Financial Officer from September 2010 to March 2017, CFO In Training from April 2010 to September 2010, and Director of Financial Services from March 2003 to April 2010. She began her career as an Audit Senior at Ernst & Young from January 2000 to March 2003. Joyce Tamori earned a Bachelor's Degree in Business Administration in Accounting from the University of Hawai'i - Shidler College of Business between 1995 and 1999. She is also a Certified Public Accountant. In 2016, she was recognized as a member of Pacific Business News' 40 Under 40 class. She also serves on the Board of Directors at Hale Mahaolu.

Andrea Suzuki

Vice President and General Counsel

Andrea Suzuki serves as the Vice President and General Counsel at Hawaii Health Systems Corporation (HHSC). In this role, she is responsible for leading the in-house legal department, which includes managing the corporate director of risk management, a team of attorneys, and legal support staff. Suzuki provides legal counsel and healthcare risk management services for Hawaii's state-owned healthcare system and its affiliated facilities, such as acute care hospitals, critical access hospitals, long-term care facilities, and outpatient and urgent care clinics. She advises the HHSC board of directors, president and chief executive officer, and other executives on a wide range of legal and strategic business matters, including the interpretation of federal and state healthcare laws, fraud and abuse, privacy, employment, and procurement. Prior to her current position, Suzuki served as general counsel for the transfer of management and operation of Maui Memorial Medical Center, Kula Hospital, and Lanai Community Hospital from HHSC to Maui Health Systems. She also held the position of Chief Operating Officer (COO) for HHSC. Her background also includes experience in the attorney general's office, where she was involved in analyzing legislation and facilitating collaboration with legislators.
