Recent News

Jun 11, 2025
As summer begins, doctors are urging parents to schedule their children's back-to-school immunizations now to avoid delays in enrollment. Dr. Ryan Fulton, section chief of Carilion Children's Outpatient Pediatric Medicine, emphasized the importance of yearly checkups to ensure children are up to date on required vaccines according to the Virginia Department of Health guidelines. He also stressed the need for parents to rely on doctors as trusted experts when navigating online misinformation about vaccinations, highlighting that getting vaccines and necessary paperwork completed early is crucial to prevent stress and potential delays in school integration, especially for young children starting kindergarten.
Jun 09, 2025
Carilion Clinic is ending its nursing contract with Roanoke City Public Schools, a decision that has surprised some parents and local leaders. The contract is set to end in about six months. According to a letter sent to parents on May 27th, Carilion stated this decision is due to a shift in their focus towards internal pediatric care and is not a reflection of the performance of their school nurses, who they stated do exceptional work. Roanoke City Schools officials are working on determining a new staffing provider for school nursing services, with Carilion offering a six-month transition period to help ensure a smooth handoff. Other nearby school divisions, such as Roanoke County, Montgomery, and Henry counties, as well as Lynchburg City and Alleghany Highlands, directly hire their own nurses.
Jun 09, 2025
Beginning June 10th, the south entrance of the front lobby at Carilion Roanoke Memorial Hospital was scheduled to close temporarily due to scaffolding being erected as part of maintenance on the West Pavilion. Visitors were advised to use the west entrance, which reopened and was accessible via a marked safety route. Carilion Clinic recommended that visitors allow for extra time when coming to the hospital during this period. Normal traffic entrances and walkways were expected to resume around June 12th or 13th after the scaffolding work was completed. This work is the initial phase of larger maintenance planned for the West Pavilion, and further changes to traffic patterns are anticipated in the future.
Jun 06, 2025
Carilion Roanoke Memorial Hospital is implementing sidewalk updates in front of the hospital starting Friday, June 6, due to maintenance on the West Pavilion. This will involve temporary closures of parts of the sidewalk. Beginning June 5, the West Entrance of the front lobby and surrounding sidewalks will close, with pedestrians directed to the South Entrance. On June 10, the South Entrance will close and the West Entrance will reopen, accessible via a marked safety route. Regular pedestrian traffic patterns are expected to resume around June 12 or 13 once scaffolding is completed. These changes are the initial phase of larger maintenance work on the West Pavilion, and further updates to traffic patterns around the hospital are anticipated as plans are finalized. Visitors are advised to allow extra time when coming to the facility.
Jun 02, 2025
Recently retired Carilion Clinic CEO Nancy Howell Agee was inducted into the Roanoke Blacksburg Technology Council's (RBTC) Hall of Fame at their 25th anniversary TechNite celebration. Agee, who served as CEO from 2011 until her retirement last year, was recognized for her five decades of leadership in medical, biotechnology, and business fields at Carilion Clinic. Virginia Tech President Tim Sands presented the award, highlighting Agee's impact on patient care, delivery, and improvement. Additionally, Aileen Helsel from Carilion Clinic Innovation received a Regional Leadership Award at the same event.

Donald B. Halliwill

Executive Vice President and Chief Financial Officer

Donald B. Halliwill is the Executive Vice President and Chief Financial Officer at Carilion Clinic. He joined Carilion in 1997 and has over 20 years of experience in healthcare finance. Prior to his current role, which he has held since 2013, he served in various capacities within the organization, including CEO of Carilion New River Valley Medical Center from 2007 to 2010 and CFO of Carilion's hospital division. Halliwill played a key role in the reorganization of Carilion from a hospital system to a patient-centered, physician-led clinic. He earned his Bachelor of Science degree from Virginia Tech and holds an MBA from Wake Forest University's Babcock School of Business. He is a native of southwest Virginia and has a deep connection to the communities Carilion serves.

Rawle (Tony) Seupaul, MD

Executive Vice President and Chief Physician Executive

Rawle "Tony" Seupaul, MD, is the Executive Vice President and Chief Physician Executive at Carilion Clinic. He is recognized nationally for his knowledge in evidence-based medicine education and applying science to healthcare. Dr. Seupaul graduated Alpha Omega Alpha from the Honors in Medical Education Program at Northwestern University in Chicago in 1997. He completed his emergency medicine residency at Carolinas Medical Center in Charlotte, NC, where he was also chief resident. He holds a master's degree in healthcare delivery from Dartmouth College. Before joining Carilion Clinic, Dr. Seupaul was the executive associate dean for Clinical Affairs and the Stanley E. Reed endowed professor and chair of Emergency Medicine at the University of Arkansas for Medical Sciences (UAMS), starting there in 2013. He also served on the Emergency Medicine faculty at Indiana University School of Medicine and Wishard Health Services in Indianapolis. In his current role at Carilion Clinic, Dr. Seupaul is responsible for the operation of their multi-specialty physician group, which includes over 800 physicians and 300 nurse practitioners and physician assistants across 70 medical specialties. His focus is on balancing clinical practice, education, and research to ensure clinical excellence, reduce costs, and improve quality and the patient experience. He also serves on several national boards for Emergency Medicine and is an elected member of the board of directors of the Association of Academic Chairs. He is also a professor in the Department of Emergency Medicine at the Virginia Tech Carilion School of Medicine.
