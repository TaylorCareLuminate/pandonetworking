Recent News

Jun 16, 2025
The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS) is expanding Medicaid services for American Indian and Alaska Native communities by approving Medicaid State Plan Amendments (SPAs) in several states. These amendments will allow Indian Health Service and tribal clinics to receive Medicaid funding to provide care in locations beyond the traditional clinic setting, such as in patients' homes or schools, particularly benefiting those in remote areas. This development follows a June 2024 Supreme Court decision affirming that IHS must reimburse tribes for overhead costs associated with contracted health services from third parties like Medicaid and Medicare. The article also referenced a 2024 IHS contract for a new electronic health record system aimed at connecting tribal communities across multiple states, highlighting ongoing efforts to improve healthcare access and infrastructure.
Jun 13, 2025
The Acting Director of the Indian Health Service has issued a letter to Tribal Leaders and Urban Indian Organization Leaders initiating Tribal Consultation and Urban Confer. The purpose of this consultation is to solicit recommendations regarding a proposed realignment of the Indian Health Service. Additionally, the letter includes an invitation for Tribal Leaders to contribute to a display that will be installed at the IHS Headquarters located in Rockville, MD, honoring Tribal Nations. This initiative underscores IHS's commitment to engaging with tribal partners on significant organizational changes and recognizing the contributions of Tribal Nations.
Jun 13, 2025
The Indian Health Service's "In the News" section includes a recap of the May Tribal Consultation and Urban Confer. This recap highlights discussions that took place, with a specific focus on the Four Directions Warehouse. The mention of this recap on the IHS news page indicates recent communication and engagement with tribal and urban Indian organizations regarding operational matters and supply chain issues related to the warehouse. The IHS Headquarters in Rockville, MD, oversees such consultations and operational aspects.
Jun 13, 2025
The Indian Health Service (IHS) Health Information Technology (IT) Modernization Program hosted its first tribal consultation and urban confer session of 2025 on May 15, with a recap published on June 13, 2025. [3, 4] This virtual session included participants from across Indian Country and focused on the program's advancements in enterprise design activities, pilot site preparation, and the Four Directions Warehouse (4DW) initiative. [3] The 4DW is described as the program's cloud-based environment designed to store, organize, and provide secure access to historical clinical data currently held in the Resource and Patient Management System (RPMS). [3] The recap highlighted that the 4DW supports a smooth transition to the new Electronic Health Record (EHR) system, PATH EHR, by securely archiving legacy data, reducing system burden, and improving access and interoperability for informed care. [3] The session also included an open dialogue between program leadership and tribal and urban partners, addressing questions about data migration, organizational support for using 4DW, and strategies for retiring legacy systems. [3] These discussions are intended to guide future program activities and support the successful deployment of PATH EHR. [3] The IHS Health IT Modernization Program reiterated its commitment to connecting and collaborating with partners through such sessions and open forums. [3]
Jun 11, 2025
Recent policy updates as of June 11, 2025, from the National Council of Urban Indian Health (NCUIH) discussed the FY 2026 appropriations, mentioning the President's budget proposal for the Indian Health Service and Urban Indian Health. The proposal includes $8.1 billion for IHS and a flat funding level of $90.4 million for Urban Indian Health compared to FY 2025. The updates also touched upon protecting American Indian and Alaska Native people on Medicaid, with NCUIH submitting comments supporting the exemption of this population from work requirements in state Medicaid programs. While not detailing specific disputes with payers, this highlights ongoing policy discussions and advocacy efforts related to a major payer (Medicaid) and the financial stability of IHS and Urban Indian Organizations, which rely significantly on third-party revenue like Medicaid reimbursement.

Executive Leadership

Roselyn Tso

Director

An enrolled Tribal member of the Navajo Nation and a respected leader with decades of public service experience working in all levels of the Indian Health Service. Began her IHS career 38 years ago in the Yakama Service Unit in Washington State. Served in numerous leadership roles—in the Pacific Northwest and at the Indian Health Service headquarters in Rockville, Maryland. Served as the Director of the Navajo Area Indian Health Service. Demonstrated tremendous leadership in the face of some of the most harrowing days and months on the front lines of the COVID-19 pandemic. Played a key role in the vaccination campaign that has seen the Navajo Nation achieve some of the highest rates of vaccination in the entire world. Well acquainted with the long-running challenges in the Indian Health Service that in many cases stem from decades—really, centuries—of federal under-investment. Served just over two years as director of the Indian Health Service. Career at the agency spanned more than three decades, most recently as the IHS Navajo Area Director.

Chief Medical Officer

An enrolled member of the Navajo Nation. Serves as the Chief Medical Officer for the Indian Health Service. IHS’ lead expert on medical and public health topics, guiding the IHS Office of the Director and IHS staff throughout the country on American Indian and Alaska Native health care policies and issues. Began her career with the IHS as a general surgeon and served as the chief medical officer for the Navajo Area Indian Health Service from 2017-2021. Board certified in general surgery and a fellow of the American College of Surgeons. Responsible for overall patient care policy and program development, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the IHS health care delivery system. Coordinates with staff regarding health services, clinical guidelines, and planning activities, and serves as the primary liaison and advocate for IHS health professionals. Previously served as the chief medical officer at the Gallup Service Unit from 2014-2017, and also as acting IHS chief medical officer from May to June of this year (2021).
