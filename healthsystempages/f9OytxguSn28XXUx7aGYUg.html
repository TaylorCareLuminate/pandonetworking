Jun 11, 2025
The U.S. Department of Labor announced a settlement with Unum Life Insurance Co. of America, a Portland, Maine-based insurer. The settlement requires Unum to change its practices regarding the proof of good health requirement for job-based life insurance plans covered by the Employee Retirement Income Security Act (ERISA). An investigation by the department's Employee Benefits Security Administration (EBSA) found that Unum had a practice of accepting premiums without verifying if participants were insurable and subsequently denying claims after a participant's death, citing a lack of proof of insurability. EBSA also found that Unum would deny coverage to dependents based on a delayed effective date if they were found to have been disabled at the time of enrollment, without clearly informing participants of this policy. The settlement prohibits Unum from denying claims solely due to a lack of evidence of insurability when premiums were accepted and requires the company to re-process certain denied claims dating back to 2018 and 2016.

Jun 04, 2025
Contract talks have stalled between Northern Light Health, a healthcare system headquartered in Bangor, and Anthem, Maine's largest health insurer. Northern Light Health announced on June 4, 2025, that its contract with Anthem is set to expire on September 30, 2025, if an agreement is not reached regarding increased reimbursement rates. Should the contract expire, Northern Light hospitals across their service area, including in Bangor, would become out-of-network for individuals with Anthem insurance. Northern Light Health stated that Anthem's reimbursement rates have risen by less than 1.5 percent, significantly lower than the average annual inflation rate for health services, which is around 7.5 percent. The healthcare system emphasized that adequate reimbursement is crucial for maintaining the operation and staffing of hospitals, practices, and services. James Rohrbaugh, executive vice president and chief financial officer for Northern Light Health, commented that Anthem's reluctance to keep pace with the cost of providing care exacerbates the risks faced by rural healthcare providers. Northern Light Health also highlighted what they see as an increase in payment denials by Anthem for patient care and noted Anthem's 10 percent increase in profit margin in Maine. The healthcare system has committed to working with patients and group policyholders to help them understand their options for care if a contract agreement is not achieved. This situation echoes a public contract dispute in 2022 between MaineHealth, the state's largest health system, and Anthem, which was ultimately resolved before MaineHealth left Anthem's network. Executive Leadership

Sara Gagné-Holmes

Commissioner

Maine native. Sworn in as Commissioner of the Department of Health and Human Services on October 15, 2024, following bipartisan confirmation by the Maine Senate on October 10. Served in an Acting capacity for the Department since June 1, 2024, and before that was a Deputy Commissioner for DHHS for five years, from 2019-2024. As Commissioner, she leads the largest agency in state government, which provides health care and social services to approximately a third of the state's population. Prior to joining the Department, Gagné-Holmes was a senior program associate with the John T. Gorman Foundation. She was also the executive director of a statewide, nonprofit civil legal aid and advocacy organization for eight years. Gagné-Holmes' experience additionally includes serving in Gov. John Baldacci's administration as the Deputy Legal Counsel and a Senior Policy and Legal Adviser in the Office of Health Policy and Finance, working as a private attorney focused on health care law, clerking for the Maine Supreme Judicial Court, and owning and operating a café. Gagné-Holmes graduated from Bowdoin College and from the University of Maine School of Law.

Beth Hamm

Deputy Commissioner

Bethany Hamm is the Deputy Commissioner at the Maine Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS), appointed in March 2019. She has a long history with the department, serving for over 30 years in various roles, including Acting Commissioner from August 2018. Before that, she was the Director of the Office for Family Independence (OFI) for nearly five years, where she oversaw public welfare programs such as TANF, Medicaid eligibility, SNAP, Disability Determination Services, and Child Support Enforcement. Hamm is recognized for her efforts in developing policies and partnerships to promote economic stability and reduce dependence on public assistance.

Benjamin Mann

Deputy Commissioner of Finance

Benjamin Mann is the Deputy Commissioner of Finance at the Maine Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS). He has served in this role since August 2019. In this position, he is responsible for overseeing the Department's budget, which is nearly $3 billion over the biennium, as well as contract management, audit, administration and facilities, and information technology. He has played a central role in financial matters for the DHHS. Mann holds a Masters in Public Policy from Duke University and completed his undergraduate education at Colgate University. He is also the finance chair of the Maine Recovery Council, which is tasked with distributing a portion of Maine's opioid settlement funds.
