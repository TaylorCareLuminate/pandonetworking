Jun 06, 2025
In recognition of June 6 as Hospitals Against Violence Day, Valley Health reaffirmed its commitment to fostering a safe, supportive, and secure environment for every caregiver, patient, and visitor. The health system highlighted that violence in healthcare remains a significant challenge, impacting the well-being of their team and the quality of care provided. According to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, healthcare workers experience the highest rates of workplace violence of any profession. Valley Health is taking ongoing steps to strengthen safety across its facilities, including increasing on-site security presence, upgrading and replacing emergency call boxes, and establishing and communicating clear behavioral expectations for patients and families, with a zero-tolerance policy for those who violate their standards. Mark Nantz, President and CEO of Valley Health, stated that the organization is committed to creating a safer environment for all caregivers and that no one who dedicates their life to healing should face fear or harm in their workplace. May 19, 2025
Valley Health System, based in Paramus, N.J., is actively developing a hospital-at-home program with the goal of addressing capacity challenges and providing care to patients in their residences. The health system received a waiver from CMS in March to offer acute hospital care at home through The Valley Hospital in Paramus, with plans to launch the program in July. According to Toni Modak, BSN, RN, vice president of Valley Home Care, delivering acute care at home represents the future of healthcare, offering eligible patients the convenience of receiving care for common inpatient conditions within the comfort of their homes. Valley Health System has been monitoring the hospital-at-home trend since 2020 and began seriously evaluating the necessary infrastructure approximately a year ago. The decision to pursue this initiative was primarily driven by the organization's objective to improve access to care. [7] Valley Health System has partnered with Inbound Health, a company specializing in hospital-at-home services, for both the clinical and technological components of the program. Modak indicated that this care model necessitates a substantial investment, encompassing technology such as integrating virtual capabilities into Valley Health's EHR, as well as the staffing required for program setup and operation. The anticipated benefits include freeing up inpatient beds, reducing readmissions, enhancing patient outcomes, and increasing patient satisfaction. [7] Currently, Valley Health is conducting testing and tabletop exercises to ensure the program's readiness for launch, emphasizing that they will not go live until they are confident that their infrastructure is fully prepared to deliver the same quality of care as provided within their brick-and-mortar facilities. The development of this program has required extensive collaboration across all departments within the hospital, with each playing a critical role in decision-making, taking ownership of various components, and ensuring seamless integration. May 07, 2025
Valley Health System, based in Winchester, VA, announced the recipients of its 2025 Community Partnership Grants, awarding a total of $190,000 to 12 local non-profit organizations. These grants aim to support initiatives addressing critical health needs in the community, reflecting Valley Health's understanding that community well-being extends beyond clinical care. The funded projects align with Valley Health's strategies, focusing on areas such as health education, mobile health, transportation, mental health, and community collaborations. The top grant of $50,000 was awarded to the Page Alliance for Community Action (PACA) to establish a pilot program strengthening the local and fresh food network in Page and southern Shenandoah counties by connecting community gardens and producers to markets and offering Community Supported Agriculture (CSA) options.
May 06, 2025
Winchester Medical Center (WMC) has achieved its eleventh consecutive "A" Hospital Safety Grade from The Leapfrog Group, an independent national nonprofit watchdog organization focused on patient safety. This consistent high rating in the Spring 2025 Hospital Safety Grades demonstrates WMC's sustained commitment to protecting patients from errors, injuries, accidents, and infections within the hospital setting. The Leapfrog Hospital Safety Grade is a widely recognized program that assigns grades from 'A' to 'F' to nearly 3,000 general acute care hospitals across the United States based on more than 30 evidence-based performance measures related to patient safety outcomes and practices. WMC is one of only a few hospitals in Virginia to have maintained an 'A' grade consistently since Spring 2020.
