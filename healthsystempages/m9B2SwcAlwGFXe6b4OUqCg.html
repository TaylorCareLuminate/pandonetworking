Recent News

Jun 16, 2025
Building on previous efforts, Governor Hochul's office reiterated the improved compliance of Medicaid managed care plans in New York with rules for accessing behavioral health care services. The state has actively worked to ensure these plans comply with regulations and provide mandated coverage to New Yorkers. The State Office of Mental Health (OMH) specifically reviewed six nonquantitative treatment limitations, which are sometimes manipulated by plans to restrict access to necessary behavioral health care, and found all carriers to be in compliance in this specific area. However, the broader examination by OMH indicated that while Capital District Physicians' Health Plan, Inc. and Excellus BlueCross BlueShield were fully compliant with all 19 nonquantitative treatment limitations, most other managed care plans did not fully comply with other aspects of the Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act. Discrepancies were noted in rate-setting processes for behavioral health services, leading to lower provider reimbursements compared to medical and surgical services. New York State has a record of enforcement against Medicaid managed care plans for violations, including issuing 95 citations between 2019 and 2021 to all 15 plans and levying over $1 million in fines against 11 carriers. These funds were used to support the CHAMP program, which helps consumers with behavioral health insurance and access issues. Earlier reviews by OMH (2018-2020) had identified substantial inappropriate denials for specialty services claims, amounting to $39 million in a five-month period.
Jun 10, 2025
Governor Kathy Hochul announced on June 10, 2025, that all Medicaid managed care plans operating in New York State have shown improved compliance with regulations aimed at ensuring fair access to mental health and substance use disorder services. This announcement comes even as federal enforcement of these protections is being reduced. The New York State Office of Mental Health (OMH) conducted a review of these plans, specifically examining six nonquantitative treatment limitations which are sometimes used to restrict access to behavioral health care. The review found all carriers to be in compliance. Furthermore, OMH's detailed examination determined that both Capital District Physicians' Health Plan, Inc. and Excellus BlueCross BlueShield were 100 percent compliant with all 19 nonquantitative treatment limitations reviewed.
Jun 04, 2025
Governor Kathy Hochul announced the availability of $45.9 million through the Empire State Supportive Housing Initiative. This program provides funding for supportive services aimed at helping New Yorkers experiencing homelessness to find stable housing. The funding increase for this program is the first since its inception in 2016 and is part of the FY26 Enacted Budget. The significantly higher rates provided through this initiative will support units serving various populations, including adults experiencing homelessness, survivors of domestic and gender-based violence, veterans, and chronically homeless families and individuals living with a mental illness or substance use disorder. The New York State Office of Mental Health (OMH) has announced a procurement opportunity through a Request for Proposals (RFP) for a Training Curriculum & Learning Management System. This RFP, listed on the OMH Procurement Opportunities webpage, is part of Governor Hochul's multi-year initiative aimed at transforming the continuum of mental health care across New York State. Jun 03, 2025
New York State announced the availability of up to $2.7 million to develop new Flexible Assertive Community Treatment (ACT) teams. These teams are intended to serve individuals with serious mental illness (SMI) who have not been successfully engaged by traditional mental health treatment systems. Flexible ACT teams build upon the evidence-based ACT model by offering enhanced flexibility in service delivery, tailored to meet the evolving needs of individuals. The introduction of Flexible ACT demonstrates the NYS OMH's commitment to ensuring mental health services adapt to better serve the SMI population that is most disengaged from care. [12]

Executive Leadership

Dr. Ann Marie T. Sullivan, MD

Commissioner

Served as Commissioner for the New York State Office of Mental Health since 2014. She is responsible for implementing Governor Kathy Hochul's landmark plan to transform the state's mental health system and expand access to care for all New Yorkers. To date, this initiative has invested more than $2 billion into dramatically expanding services, supports, and capacity across the state. Under Dr. Sullivan's leadership, the mental health system in New York serves over 800,000 individuals each year and provides over 53,000 units of supportive housing for individuals living with mental illness. Dr. Sullivan has focused on ensuring integrated care throughout the mental health system, addressing the stigma around mental health, fostering equity and inclusion and incorporating the presence of peers with lived experience throughout the service system. A few major initiatives during her tenure include the implementation of 988 and expansion of crisis services across the state, a focus on school based mental health services for our youth and a comprehensive engagement and housing program for individuals with serious mental illness living unsheltered in the community. Dr. Sullivan previously served as senior vice president for the Queens Health Network of New York City Health and Hospitals, and Director of Psychiatry at Elmhurst and Queens Hospitals where she developed a comprehensive inpatient and ambulatory psychiatric service system, youth programs, substance use services, mobile outreach, and school-based programs. A native of Queens, Dr. Sullivan graduated from New York University's Washington Square College and it's School of Medicine. She completed her Psychiatric Residency at New York University/Bellevue Hospital.

Thomas Smith, MD

Chief Medical Officer

Oversees clinical and quality aspects of the New York State public mental health system with a focus on improving access to prevention, recovery, and rehabilitation services for persons with serious mental illness (SMI). He is the recipient of numerous NIMH and foundation grants for studies of engagement strategies for persons with SMI, services for persons with first episode psychosis, and care management approaches for high need persons with SMI. Dr. Smith is also Special Lecturer in the Department of Psychiatry at Columbia University.
