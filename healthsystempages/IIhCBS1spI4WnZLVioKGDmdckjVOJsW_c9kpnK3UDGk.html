Recent News

Jun 11, 2025
The Colorado Department of Human Services has launched a new pilot project within the SNAP program. This initiative allows Coloradans to receive a bonus when purchasing healthy foods like fruits and vegetables at participating retailers. The program provides an automatic dollar-for-dollar match on their EBT cards, up to $60 per month, which can be used for any SNAP-eligible food. A key feature is that this rebate is applied electronically and does not require a separate application or vouchers, streamlining the process for participants. According to Abby McClelland, director of the Food and Energy Assistance Division at CDHS, Colorado is one of three states administering this program, which aims to help families stretch their food budget and access healthy options, especially given recent increases in grocery prices. The program is currently available at over 30 locations across Colorado, with a focus on smaller retailers, farmers markets, and stores in rural areas to support the local economy, and there are plans to expand the number of participating retailers.

May 12, 2025
Recent changes and communication issues within Colorado's Medicaid program have led to mental health providers expressing feelings of "betrayal" regarding promised enhanced reimbursement rates. Following a state initiative to reform the behavioral health system and transition away from long-standing contracts with large regional mental health centers, independent clinics were encouraged to develop new programs and were offered higher Medicaid reimbursement rates under an "essential safety net provider" designation. However, the state Medicaid department (Colorado Department of Health Care Policy and Financing - HCPF) has reportedly withdrawn its directive requiring the regional agencies that process claims (Regional Accountable Entities - RAEs) to continue paying these enhanced rates. This has resulted in some clinics, despite receiving the "essential" designation from the Behavioral Health Administration, being rejected by their regional RAEs or facing uncertainty about future payment rates after July 1, 2025. Providers who invested in developing new services based on the promise of higher rates are now concerned about the financial viability of these programs, highlighting a significant dispute between healthcare providers and the state's Medicaid administration and its contracted payment entities.
