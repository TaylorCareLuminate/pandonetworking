Recent News

Jun 02, 2025
The Monroe County Health Care Authority, which operates Monroe County Hospital in Monroeville, Alabama, is considering implementing a swing bed program. This proposed initiative would involve converting 10 existing acute-care beds into skilled nursing facility beds. The purpose of a swing bed program is to allow patients to transition from acute hospital care to a skilled nursing level of care without needing to be transferred to a different facility. This can be particularly beneficial in rural areas. The project is estimated to cost $345,000 and requires a certificate of need. Interested parties have until July 14, 2025, to submit written statements or appear before the Certificate of Need Review Board.

Nov 27, 2024
Nathan Tudor was recently named chief executive officer of Monroe County Hospital in Monroeville, AL. His appointment is aimed at better serving the healthcare needs of local residents. Tudor's key areas of focus include strengthening the hospital's services, encouraging collaboration among the hospital team, and ensuring the hospital adapts to the changing healthcare needs of the community. USA Health and Monroe County Hospital have a management services agreement in place since 2021. Tudor brings prior experience from rural healthcare environments and is expected to guide the hospital towards growth and innovation. He has stated his commitment to working closely with the hospital staff, patients, and the broader community to improve the quality of care and believes in the strong connection between a healthcare system and the community it serves, indicating his intention to become actively involved in the Monroeville community.
May 28, 2024
Monroe County Health Care Authority, which operates Monroe County Hospital in Monroeville, AL, adopted new accounting guidance in 2023 for Subscription-Based Information Technology Arrangements (GASB Statement No. 96). According to their financial statements for the years ended September 30, 2023 and 2022, the Authority entered into subscription-based information technology arrangements totaling $225,000 in 2023 and $486,000 in 2022. This indicates an ongoing investment in and reliance on IT subscriptions to support the hospital's operations and services. The adoption of this new accounting standard reflects the increasing prevalence of these types of IT arrangements in healthcare. [5]
Mar 01, 2024
According to information from the Monroe County Hospital website's news section, Aletheia Health Partners (AHP) partnered with Monroe County Hospital, leading to a significant turnaround for the hospital over the past 12 months. The hospital, which was reportedly close to closure due to financial losses, low morale, and a declining reputation, has seen improvements in patient care, including achieving new benchmarks in emergency care quality and earning accreditations. There has also been investment in property improvements, such as equipment upgrades and new installations. Two facility improvement plans, including potential remodels and additions to separate inpatient and outpatient services or a comprehensive first-floor remodel with expanded areas and a new entrance, are under consideration to modernize care and improve the patient experience. The partnership and subsequent improvements are presented as charting a bold new path for healthcare in Monroe County.

Executive Leadership

Kerry Trapnell

Interim Chief Executive Officer

Kerry Trapnell serves as the Interim Chief Executive Officer of Monroe County Hospital. He is also the Chief Administrative Officer for Aletheia Health Partners, Inc., a company that provides strategic advisory services, interim leadership, and hospital management to rural hospitals, and manages the daily operations of Monroe County Hospital. Trapnell has over 25 years of experience in rural healthcare, holding various leadership positions such as CFO, CEO, and Consultant in Critical Access, Acute Care, and Post-Acute Care hospitals. His work has focused on financial turnarounds through service growth and revenue generation to maintain healthcare access in rural communities. He recently led the first successful Critical Access Hospital conversion in Georgia in nearly 20 years. His previous work has earned him recognition, including CEO of the Year and Hospital of the Year awards, as well as being named Georgia's Community Star by the National Organization of State Offices of Rural Health. Hospitals he has led have also received awards for patient quality, including being in the top 10% in the nation for Patient Outcomes multiple times. Trapnell has also served on numerous community and non-profit boards.

Megan Randall, R.T.(R)(ARRT)

Chief Operating Officer

Megan Randall, R.T.(R)(ARRT), serves as the Chief Operating Officer at Monroe County Hospital. She also currently holds the position of Chief Nursing Officer at the hospital and is a longtime employee there. Randall earned a Bachelor's Degree in Radiologic Sciences and is registered with the American Registry of Radiologic Technologists (ARRT) with an (R) certification.

Casey Fleckenstein, BSN, RN

Chief Nursing Officer

Casey Fleckenstein, BSN, RN, is the Chief Nursing Officer and Hospital Administrator at Monroe County Hospital. She began her nursing career at Monroe County Hospital and has worked there for over 20 years, holding various positions including staff nurse in Medical Surgical, Emergency Department, and Post Anesthesia Care Units, as well as Medical Surgical Manager, Emergency Room Manager, Case Management/Swing Bed Coordinator, and Emergency Management Coordinator. Casey earned her Associate of Science in Nursing from Gordon College in 2001 and later completed her Bachelor of Science in Nursing at Western Governor's University in 2019. She is also a Certified Lean Six Sigma Green Belt and has used these strategies to improve service excellence, patient throughput, patient safety, and accreditation success. Born and raised in Monroe County, she is deeply passionate about the hospital and her community.
