Jun 16, 2025
UNC Health is responding to a social media post by North Carolina Governor Josh Stein that suggested UNC Health Rockingham, an Eden-based hospital, was among five rural hospitals at risk of closure under a proposed congressional budget. UNC Health issued a statement to WFMY News 2 asserting that UNC Health Rockingham is not in danger of closing and that UNC Health remains committed to keeping its hospitals across the state open to provide patient care. The health system also noted that the report referenced by Governor Stein was created without consultation from UNC Health leadership, and they were not given an opportunity to review and comment on the status of the UNC Health hospitals mentioned. While acknowledging that federal budget cuts, particularly to Medicaid, could negatively impact rural hospitals nationwide, UNC Health stated that hospitals owned by large health systems like itself are better protected against the risk of closure. Jun 11, 2025
A North Carolina federal judge has ruled that the University of North Carolina's hospital system must face a discrimination lawsuit filed by a fired surgical resident. The resident, John Espey III, alleges his discharge was motivated by bias against his mental health conditions and in retaliation for challenging the system's tendency to overwork surgical residents. U.S. Circuit Judge Toby Heytens, sitting by designation, deemed Espey's Americans with Disabilities Act claims strong enough to survive UNC Health Care System's motion to dismiss. The decision allows Espey to continue arguing that UNC Health Care System fired him and reported him to the North Carolina Medical Board due to a distorted view of his depression, anxiety, and attention deficit disorder, as well as in retaliation for raising concerns about overwork that exacerbated his conditions. The lawsuit names both UNC Health Care System and Espey's former supervisor as defendants.
Jun 10, 2025
Chapel Hill-based UNC Health has filed lawsuits against Aetna and Humana in a North Carolina federal court, alleging the insurers underpaid for drugs purchased through the federal 340B drug discount program between 2018 and 2022. UNC Health claims the payers improperly reduced reimbursement rates for 340B drugs provided at UNC hospitals by applying a federal payment reduction policy that was later deemed unlawful by the U.S. Supreme Court in 2022. The health system is seeking restitution for the alleged underpayments and is demanding that both insurers honor their contractual obligations under their Medicare Advantage agreements. UNC is requesting a judgment to compel the insurers to reimburse the full amount allegedly owed, plus additional damages exceeding $75,000, and a court order to ensure future compliance with the correct payment structure. Aetna denies the allegations and intends to defend itself, while Humana declined to comment on the pending litigation.
