Jun 10, 2025
A proposed federal budget bill that recently passed the U.S. House of Representatives could result in nearly $800 billion in Medicaid cuts over the next decade. This bill includes potential changes such as a work requirement for some recipients and more frequent eligibility verification. Healthcare advocates and providers, including Penn Highlands Healthcare, have expressed significant concerns about the potential impact of these cuts on rural hospitals and the ability of patients to access care. Douglas Winner, chief financial officer for Penn Highlands Healthcare, stated that the system is "greatly concerned" about the proposed cuts. [22, 23] He highlighted that rural hospitals are already facing substantial financial challenges due to increased costs for labor, drugs, and supplies, coupled with insufficient reimbursement rates and ongoing staffing shortages. [22, 23] The potential loss of Medicaid coverage for a significant number of Pennsylvanians, estimated to be over 300,000 by the state's Department of Human Services, could further exacerbate these financial struggles and potentially force rural hospitals to reduce services or close. [22, 23]

May 29, 2025
Penn Highlands State College has partnered with the Sexual Assault Forensic Examination-Telehealth (SAFE-T) System to improve sexual assault care in the Centre County region. This collaboration allows Penn Highlands State College to offer 24/7 access to board-certified sexual assault nurse examiners (SANEs) through specialized forensic telehealth technology. This is particularly beneficial for rural areas where access to highly trained SANEs is often limited. The SAFE-T System enables a telehealth SANE (teleSANE) to remotely observe a live examination, providing guidance to the on-site nurse and ensuring best practices, proper evidence collection, and a supportive environment for the patient. The technology also supports secure, high-resolution photo evidence collection with automatic transfer to a secure cloud.

Penn Highlands DuBois was one of only a dozen hospitals in Pennsylvania to receive a 2025 Achievement Award from the Hospital and Healthsystem Association of Pennsylvania (HAP). The hospital was specifically honored with the Workforce Innovator Award. This recognition highlights Penn Highlands DuBois's investment in the professional growth of its nurses and the successful implementation of decision-making strategies that have led to reduced vacancy rates and improved quality outcomes in a rural setting. This award follows Penn Highlands DuBois receiving the Pathway to Excellence designation from the American Nurses Credentialing Center (ANCC) in late 2024, which acknowledges a positive practice environment and a culture of sustained excellence for nurses.
