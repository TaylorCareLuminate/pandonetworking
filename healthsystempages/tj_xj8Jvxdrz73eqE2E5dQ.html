Recent News

May 05, 2025
Roche Diagnostics Corporation filed a lawsuit against Insight Foundation of Trumbull in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Ohio, seeking nearly $300,000 for unpaid expenses. Roche Diagnostics provides medical tests and tools and had an existing agreement with Steward Health Care, the former owner of Trumbull Regional Hospital and Hillside Rehabilitation Hospital in Ohio. According to the lawsuit, after Insight Foundation took over management of these hospitals following Steward's bankruptcy, a Michigan-based affiliate of Insight Foundation informed Roche that Insight would be responsible for hospital expenses starting in September 2024. Roche claims it sent invoices to Insight Foundation for goods and services, but these invoices totaling $294,646.55 have not been paid. This lawsuit adds to the legal challenges faced by Insight following the abrupt closure of the two Ohio hospitals in late March 2025, which also led to widespread staff furloughs and impacted hundreds of healthcare workers. Apr 15, 2025
Phillips Medical Capital, LLC filed an emergency motion in court regarding the sale of Trumbull Regional Medical Center and Hillside Rehabilitation Hospital, the two Ohio hospitals that were acquired by Insight Health System and subsequently closed. Phillips Medical Capital claimed they had leased medical equipment to both hospitals in 2019 and that this equipment could not be sold without being returned to them. They alleged that since the hospitals' closure in March, some equipment had been removed and Insight had not been communicative regarding the return of their property. A judge granted the motion the same day, ordering Insight to allow Phillips to retrieve their equipment from the hospitals within five days. This legal action is related to the fallout from the hospitals' closure, which Insight blamed on a lack of funding from the previous owner, Steward Health Care. [12]

Apr 10, 2025
Insight Health, a company specializing in AI-powered healthcare solutions, announced a strategic partnership with Office Practicum, a provider of pediatric-specific electronic health record (EHR) solutions. This collaboration aims to integrate Insight Health's advanced AI technology with Office Practicum's expertise and clinical dataset to create a specialized AI documentation assistant tailored for pediatric workflows. The partnership is launching "Opie AI," a white-labeled version of Insight Health's AI scribe technology for pediatricians, with the goal of significantly reducing documentation time and allowing providers to focus more on patient care. Opie AI is designed with a pediatric-specific focus, aligning with familiar language and formatting used in children's healthcare. Future updates are expected to include specialized capabilities addressing the unique needs of pediatric care, such as understanding growth milestones, age-specific vital signs, and immunization schedules. Insight Health's AI platform is adaptable to various medical specialties, and this partnership highlights its white-labeling capabilities for partners to offer AI solutions under their own brand. Opie AI is scheduled to be available to Office Practicum customers starting May 2025, with more features planned for later in the year, including enhanced documentation, clinical decision support, and workflow automation. The technical integration aims for a seamless experience for clinicians adopting AI.
Apr 03, 2025
Insight Health System is facing a federal class action lawsuit in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Ohio. The lawsuit was filed by David McCullough, a terminated employee of Insight Rehabilitation Hospital Hillside, on behalf of himself and other employees at Insight's Trumbull County facilities, including Insight Hospital Medical Center Trumbull and Insight Rehabilitation Center Hillside. The core of the complaint is the alleged failure of Insight to provide employees with a 60-day advance written notice of termination, as required by the Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification (WARN) Act. The lawsuit claims that employees were initially told they were temporarily furloughed and would remain on payroll to receive accrued vacation time, but they were not paid for this time or for at least three weeks of wages prior to termination. Additionally, health insurance benefits were allegedly terminated abruptly despite deductions from paychecks. The lawsuit seeks lost wages and benefits for the 60-day notice period, as well as other damages, and names Insight Foundation of Hillside, Insight Foundation of Trumbull, and Firdaus Foundation (identified as a Michigan-based nonprofit operating as Insight Foundation of America and owner of Insight Health System) as defendants. Insight Health System did not respond to requests for comment regarding the lawsuit. [17, 18, 22]
