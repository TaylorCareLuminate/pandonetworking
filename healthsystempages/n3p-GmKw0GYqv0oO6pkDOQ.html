Jun 08, 2025
Tallahassee is currently facing a significant nursing shortage, a situation mirrored across much of Florida. The healthcare sector constitutes a substantial portion of Tallahassee's economy, employing over 14,000 individuals in medical and related fields as of 2023. Despite this, the demand for nurses continues to outpace the supply. Projections from the National Institutes of Health indicate a need for over 275,000 additional nurses nationwide by 2030, with Florida alone estimated to require around 60,000 more nurses. While job opportunities for nurses are expected to grow faster than in other occupations, obstacles such as students' preparedness for the rigorous education and a deficit of nursing educators are contributing to the shortage. Retention is also a concern, although data from the Florida Hospital Association shows improvement in retention rates since the COVID-19 pandemic exacerbated the issue. Hospitals, including Tallahassee Memorial Hospital, are working on strategies to improve the work environment, retention, and recruitment efforts. Discussions are ongoing about how to address the patient flow problem and the lack of sufficient resources to meet demand.
Jun 07, 2025
A town hall meeting was convened by local Democrats to discuss the ongoing situation surrounding Tallahassee Memorial HealthCare, particularly the potential sale of the hospital to Florida State University. The discussion involved local commissioners and the Leon County Democratic Party Chair, focusing on the implications of changing the ownership and governance of what is considered a vital community asset. Concerns were raised about the transparency of the ongoing negotiations and whether a potential transition to university ownership would best serve the interests of Tallahassee residents, especially those who rely on the hospital for care regardless of their ability to pay. This public forum highlights the significant community interest and debate surrounding the future of TMH.
Jun 05, 2025
A town hall meeting was held on the evening of June 5, 2025, where local elected officials and residents gathered to discuss concerns regarding a potential sale of Tallahassee Memorial Healthcare property. The discussion was attended by Tallahassee City Commissioners Jeremy Matlow and Jack Porter, and Leon County Commissioners David O'Keefe and Bill Proctor, with Leon County Democratic Party chair Ryan Ray moderating. The possibility of selling the community-owned hospital arose after city leaders indicated they might consider such a sale following a proposal from TMH to restructure its governance. Florida State University has expressed interest in purchasing the hospital to bring it under the FSU Health umbrella and advance the creation of an academic medical center. Many attendees and participating officials voiced strong sentiment for keeping the hospital under local ownership, raising concerns that selling to the university could result in TMH becoming a state operation. The town hall served as a platform for the progressive wing of local officials and residents to voice their opposition and call for a broader community conversation about the hospital's future.

Jun 01, 2025
Effective June 1, Tallahassee Memorial HealthCare (TMH) implemented a new executive leadership structure. This change signifies a transformation from a single hospital into a comprehensive, regionally focused health system. The strategic shift is intended to ensure TMH continues providing access to high-quality healthcare services to meet the evolving needs of the communities it serves. As part of this restructuring, Mark O'Bryant will remain Chief Executive Officer of Tallahassee Memorial HealthCare, Inc., the parent company. Ryan Smith will serve as President and Chief Operating Officer of Tallahassee Memorial Hospital, overseeing operational responsibilities in the Tallahassee market. Andrew Starr will become President of the West Market, responsible for the construction, development, and future operations of FSU Health Panama City Beach Hospital. [2, 3]

Executive Leadership

G. Mark O'Bryant

President and CEO

G. Mark O'Bryant is President and Chief Executive Officer of Tallahassee Memorial HealthCare, Inc., the parent corporation of Tallahassee Memorial Hospital. He assumed the role in July 2003. Since arriving at Tallahassee Memorial, Mr. O'Bryant has focused on clinical, operational and financial improvements centered on the development of a patient and family-centered care model. The organization has been restructured around eight clinical service lines. He has also coordinated the development and implementation of a metrics-driven strategic plan. Prior to his recruitment to Tallahassee, Mr. O'Bryant came from Memorial Health Care System, Chattanooga, Tennessee, where he served as Sr. Vice President and Chief Operating Officer. Mr. O'Bryant is a Diplomat in the American College of Health Care Executives. His undergraduate degree is from Brigham Young University; his Master of Health Administration and Master of Business Administration degrees are from Georgia State University. [2]
