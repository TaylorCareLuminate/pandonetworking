Recent News

Jun 09, 2025
The Georgia Department of Behavioral Health and Developmental Disabilities (DBHDD), in partnership with River Edge Behavioral Health and Mercer University School of Medicine, announced the opening of the state's first Crisis Services and Diagnostic Center (CSDC) for Intellectual and Developmental Disabilities (IDD) and the Mercer University School of Medicine Center for IDD Care in Macon. The facility is designed to provide specialized crisis services and comprehensive medical care for adults with IDD from across Georgia, aiming to reduce the number of individuals with IDD who end up in emergency rooms due to lack of appropriate care. The 34,000-square-foot center, located at 750 Hazel Street, cost $23.7 million and will offer a range of services including behavioral health assessments, psychiatry, neurology, dentistry, and various therapies. It also features a 16-bed crisis services unit and will serve as a training site for future medical professionals to gain experience in treating individuals with developmental disabilities. The center began accepting patients on June 16, 2025. Commissioner Kevin Tanner highlighted the facility as a significant step in meeting the needs of the IDD community and a model for collaborative public and private efforts.
Jun 01, 2025
The Georgia Department of Behavioral Health and Developmental Disabilities (DBHDD) updated its Community Behavioral Health Provider Manual, with the latest version effective July 1, 2025, and posted on June 1, 2025. This manual serves as a critical addendum to provider contracts, outlining the department's expectations, requirements, and standards for community behavioral health service providers. These standards include procedures related to eligibility, service authorization, service definitions, billing, and reporting requirements. Updates in this manual, such as revisions to service definitions and documentation requirements, directly impact how providers interact with the state's system for reimbursement, which involves payers like Medicaid. Changes noted in recent updates include removing references to older systems and incorporating new platforms like the Crisis Safety Platform, indicating ongoing adjustments to the operational aspects of provider-payer interactions.

May 20, 2025
Recent discussions at the Rosalynn Carter Georgia Mental Health Forum highlighted ongoing challenges with mental health coverage in Georgia, despite the passage of the 2022 Mental Health Parity Act. Georgia's DBHDD Commissioner Kevin Tanner noted that problems persist because individuals are often unaware of their rights and the system for reporting violations is complex. He emphasized the need for increased reporting of violations to influence the behavior of insurance companies. The 2022 act aimed to require insurance companies to cover mental health and substance abuse care comparably to physical health care, aligning with federal requirements. Enforcement for Medicaid, the state health benefit plan, and PeachCare for Kids falls under the Department of Community Health, while the Department of Insurance oversees private insurers. Experts indicated that the law's implementation is still in its early stages, and a major issue remains the insufficient network of in-network mental health providers maintained by insurance companies, which can lead to patients paying out-of-pocket or not receiving care.
May 01, 2025
The Georgia Department of Behavioral Health and Developmental Disabilities (DBHDD) implemented several new and updated information technology and security policies effective May 1, 2025. These policies cover a range of areas critical to modern IT operations and data governance within the department. Notably, new policies include guidelines on the Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) (Policy # 23-303), Information Security Incident Discovery and Reporting (23-223), and Continuous Vulnerability Management (23-226). Other updated policies address the Use of Cloud-Based Collaboration Platforms (23-227), Data and Asset Categorization and Impact Levels (23-228), Sensitive Data Breach Response and Reporting (23-230), and Information System Account Management (23-234), among others, indicating a focus on strengthening the department's IT security posture and establishing guidelines for emerging technologies like AI. These policies are part of the DBHDD PolicyStat Index for April 2025. [16]

Executive Leadership

Kevin Tanner

Commissioner

Kevin Tanner is the Commissioner of the Georgia Department of Behavioral Health and Developmental Disabilities, appointed by Governor Brian Kemp. He has over three decades of public service experience. Prior to his current role, Tanner served as the County Manager of Forsyth County. He also represented House District 9 in the Georgia House of Representatives for four terms. During his time in the House, he sponsored the founding legislation for the Behavioral Health Reform and Innovation Commission and was later appointed its Chair by Governor Kemp. This commission was instrumental in the creation of the Georgia Mental Health Parity Act (House Bill 1013). Earlier in his career, Tanner was the Dawson County Manager and spent nearly two decades at the Dawson Sheriff's Office, including 11 years as Chief Deputy/Undersheriff. He began his public service as a volunteer firefighter. Tanner is recognized for his work, having been named the Appointed Official of the Year by the Georgia Association of County Commissioners in 2011 and one of Georgia Trend Magazine's "40 Under 40" in 2007. He holds an undergraduate degree from North Georgia College and State University and a Master of Public Administration from Columbus State University.
