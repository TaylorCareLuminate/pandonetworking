Jun 16, 2025
A defamation lawsuit filed by former physician William Husel against Mount Carmel Health System and its parent company, Trinity Health, was dismissed on Monday, June 16, 2025. Franklin County Common Pleas Judge Stephen McIntosh issued a "directed verdict" in favor of Mount Carmel before the case went to a jury. The judge determined that Husel had not presented sufficient evidence to prove his case. Husel had sued for defamation and breach of contract, seeking over $18 million, alleging the health systems orchestrated a campaign to ruin his reputation. Husel's attorney expressed disappointment with the decision, stating it prevented the jury from making a decision after weeks of evidence and testimony and that they are evaluating all legal options, including appeal. Mount Carmel issued a statement saying they were pleased with the judge's decision. This lawsuit followed Husel's acquittal in 2022 on murder charges related to the deaths of patients under his care, after which he surrendered his medical license and filed multiple civil lawsuits against his former employer. Jun 10, 2025
The Ohio State University Wexner Medical Center and Mount Carmel East are collaborating on a new research study known as the CAVALIER trial (CAlcium and VAsopressin following Injury Early Resuscitation). [1, 9] This trial aims to investigate whether the early administration of calcium, vasopressin, or a combination of both can improve survival rates in severely injured trauma patients experiencing significant blood loss. [1, 9] The study is designed as an Exception from Informed Consent (EFIC) trial, which permits potentially life-saving interventions to be performed on patients who are too critically injured to provide consent. [1, 9] Consent for continued participation is sought from patients or their family members as soon as possible. [1, 9] The CAVALIER trial plans to enroll approximately 1,050 adults between the ages of 18 and 90 who have sustained traumatic injuries with substantial blood loss and are being transported to or receiving emergency surgery at a participating site like Mount Carmel East. [1, 9]

Ohio State University Wexner Medical Center and Mount Carmel East are conducting a collaborative research study, the CAlcium and VAsopressin following Injury Early Resuscitation (CAVALIER) trial. [1, 3, 5] This trial is designed to determine if administering calcium, vasopressin, or both early in the treatment of severely injured patients with significant blood loss can improve their chances of survival. [1, 5] The study is an Exception from Informed Consent (EFIC) trial, meaning it can enroll patients who are unable to give permission due to the severity of their injuries, with consent for continued participation obtained later if possible. [1] The trial is expected to include around 1,050 participants aged 18 to 90. [1] This research partnership highlights ongoing efforts between major Columbus healthcare institutions to advance trauma care. [1, 5]
Jun 04, 2025
Opening statements began on Wednesday, June 4, 2025, in the defamation trial brought by former Mount Carmel doctor William Husel against Mount Carmel Health System, its parent company Trinity Health, and former CEO Edward Lamb. Husel is seeking over $18 million in damages, claiming the health system and executives orchestrated a media campaign with false statements and press releases to portray him as a killer, despite allegedly knowing the claims were untrue. Mount Carmel's defense argues their public statements were legally protected opinions or truthful reports based on their internal investigation. They contend that Husel must prove more than just negative statements to establish defamation and that they never called him a criminal or killer. Husel was acquitted in 2022 of 14 counts of murder after being accused of overprescribing fentanyl to dying patients, though he had initially faced 25 murder charges which were later reduced. Mount Carmel has previously settled several civil suits related to the claims against Husel for a total of $12 million. Husel has surrendered his medical license. Jury selection for this trial was underway earlier in the week, starting on Monday, June 2.
