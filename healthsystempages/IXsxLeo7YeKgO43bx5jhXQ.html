May 06, 2025
TMC Health recently celebrated Nurses Week, an annual event honoring its more than 2,000 nurses. The week included various events and festivities, such as an awards ceremony. As part of the recognition, TMC Health announced that sixteen of its nurses would be recognized as Fabulous 50 winners at the upcoming Tucson Nurses Week Foundation Gala. This award celebrates 50 outstanding nurses in the Tucson area, and TMC Health is proud that 32% of the awardees work within their system. According to Joy Upshaw, TMC's chief nursing officer, this recognition highlights the commitment, passion, and expertise of their team members and their dedication to advancing healthcare and providing exceptional community service. The winners were nominated by peers and community members and selected based on criteria including role modeling, mentoring, concern for humanity, and contributions to the Tucson community. An opening ceremony on May 5 featured a procession and presentation by the Southern Arizona Nurse Honor Guard. [2]
May 06, 2025
Jennifer Mendrzycki, President and CEO of TMC Health, along with Alison Flynn Gaffney, CEO of Banner – University Medicine southern Arizona, will serve as co-chairs for the 2026 American Heart Association Heart Walk in Southern Arizona. This initiative aims to create a community of individuals trained in lifesaving skills. As co-chairs, Mendrzycki and Flynn Gaffney will mobilize civic and business leaders throughout Southern Arizona to encourage participation in the Heart Walk, a year-round campaign dedicated to fundraising for the fight against cardiovascular disease. The campaign also focuses on educating the public about hands-only CPR and providing resources for responding to cardiac emergencies. The American Heart Association recently marked 100 years of service, with a strong emphasis on hands-only CPR education as a crucial component of the Heart Walk. The organization highlights that a significant majority of people who experience cardiac arrest outside of a hospital do not survive, but immediate CPR can significantly increase their chances. Mendrzycki, Flynn Gaffney, and other regional leaders are committed to increasing the number of people in Southern Arizona prepared to act effectively in these critical situations. Marc Acuña, executive director of the American Heart Association in Tucson, stated that the Heart Walk provides a significant opportunity to address a vital community health issue, as cardiac arrest can occur unexpectedly, and bystander action is often critical for survival.
