Recent News

Jun 02, 2025
Advocate Health has launched the first phase of its new innovation district in Charlotte, North Carolina, known as The Pearl. This development is anchored by a second campus for the Wake Forest University School of Medicine and includes the North American headquarters for the surgical training institute IRCAD, along with lab space for medical devices, biomedical engineering, and life sciences. [13] Advocate Health CEO Gene Woods stated that the district is intended to transform the healthcare industry through enhanced training and research, creating an ecosystem for cutting-edge research and medical education. [13] The initial phase, which recently opened, represents an investment exceeding $1 billion and covers approximately 700,000 square feet. [13] This three-phase project is a partnership between Advocate's Atrium Health and Wexford Science & Technology and is projected to generate over 5,500 on-site jobs and more than 11,500 total jobs in the Charlotte area. [13] The district will also feature retail and housing options. [13] Other partners involved include medical technology companies such as Boston Scientific, Stryker, and Siemens Healthineers. [13] Woods highlighted that Charlotte was the largest U.S. city without a four-year medical school, and the Wake Forest University School of Medicine will welcome its first class of students this summer with a curriculum expected to be driven by artificial intelligence with a strong focus on community engagement. [13]
Jun 02, 2025
Advocate Health, headquartered in Charlotte, NC, reported its first-quarter 2025 financial results, showing a significant increase in operating income. The system recorded an operating income of $329.1 million (3.6% operating margin) for the three months ending March 31, 2025, a substantial rise from $103.7 million (1.3% margin) during the same period in the previous year. Total operating revenue reached $9.2 billion, up from $8.1 billion year-over-year, with patient service revenue accounting for $7.8 billion. Despite increased expenses, including salaries, wages, benefits, supplies, and drugs, the system's operating performance improved. Advocate Health's net income for the quarter was $499.5 million, down from $686.6 million in the first quarter of 2024. [19]
May 28, 2025
Advocate Health and The Joint Commission have announced a collaboration aimed at improving patient safety and the quality of care across Advocate Health's 69 hospitals. [20] This partnership seeks to implement significant changes by utilizing performance data for continuous improvement, fostering innovation, and increasing efficiencies throughout the health system. [20] Advocate Health is pursuing systemwide hospital accreditation through this collaboration, aiming to establish a new industry benchmark for consistently delivering high-quality care across all its facilities and building upon its existing legacy of excellence. [20] These initiatives demonstrate the organization's ongoing commitment to providing the safest and highest-quality care to both its patients and the communities it serves. [20] A systemwide approach to accreditation offers benefits such as allowing for comparisons between hospitals within the Advocate Health system, as well as benchmarking against peer groups with similar demographic profiles. [20] The Joint Commission's benchmarking tool provides both a system-level and hospital-specific view, enabling healthcare professionals to easily identify areas of strong performance and target areas for improvement. [20] Dr. Betty Chu, Chief Medical Officer at Advocate Health, emphasized that by aligning the organization around a unified set of evidence-based standards, they are empowering their entire team to drive improvements for everyone, allowing people to spend more time with their families and in meaningful pursuits. [20]
May 22, 2025
Advocate Health Care is set to open a new $10 million immediate care center on the South Side of Chicago. The plans for the center, which will be located at their existing Imani Village outpatient care facility on 95th street, were expected to be released on May 23, 2025. According to Advocate Health Care, the development of this center was influenced by community input and represents a significant step in their commitment to creating a new healthcare model on the South Side. The new facility is anticipated to offer more appointments, enhanced services, and reduced wait times for patients. Renderings of the center were made public on May 20, 2025. This investment is part of Advocate Health's larger $1 billion commitment aimed at addressing the 30-year disparity in life expectancy by increasing access to care in the South Side community. [16]
May 22, 2025
Charlotte, N.C.-based Advocate Health was one of four health systems that invested in an $8 million funding round for Rad AI, a startup developing AI technology for radiologists. Rad AI offers a generative AI solution aimed at automating parts of radiology reports, pulling findings from prior reports, and coordinating follow-up care. This investment contributes to Rad AI's total Series C funding of $68 million. Advocate Health, along with the other investing health systems, will also be collaborating with Rad AI on the development and adoption of this technology. According to Andy Crowder, chief digital and AI officer at Advocate Health, the integration of AI into radiologists' workflows is expected to improve reporting efficiency, accuracy, and quality while reducing burnout.

Brad Clark

EVP, Chief Financial Officer

Brad Clark is the Executive Vice President and Chief Financial Officer at Advocate Health. He was promoted to this role, effective December 1, 2023, after serving as interim CFO since August 2023. Clark has over two decades of experience in healthcare financial management. His responsibilities at Advocate Health include overseeing revenue cycle management, financial planning, supply chain management, financial reporting, accounting, treasury, and managed health resources. He is part of Advocate Health's executive leadership team and reports directly to the CEOs. Clark holds a bachelor's degree in finance from Appalachian State University and an MBA from Wake Forest University.

Kinneil Coltman

DHA; EVP, Chief Consumer & Social Impact Officer

Kinneil Coltman, DHA, is the Executive Vice President and Chief Consumer & Social Impact Officer at Advocate Health, one of the largest non-profit health systems in the United States. She has over 20 years of experience in the healthcare industry, with a significant focus on leading health equity and diversity initiatives. Her areas of oversight include Community Health, Community Engagement & Corporate Responsibility, Government Affairs, Social Strategy & Impact, Diversity, Equity & Inclusion, Executive Communications, and the Advocate National Center for Health Equity. Dr. Coltman holds a Bachelor of Science in Business Administration from the University of North Carolina at Asheville, a Master's in Cross Cultural Studies from the University of Houston – Clear Lake, and a Doctorate of Health Administration from the Medical University of South Carolina. She was also a Fellow in the American Hospital Association's Cross-Cultural Leadership Fellowship. Her research interests lie in interventions promoting equity within healthcare delivery systems.

Julie A. Freischlag

MD, FACS, FRCSEd(Hon), DFSVS, MAMSE; CEO and Chief Academic Officer, Atrium Health Wake Forest Baptist, Chief Academic Officer and EVP, Advocate Health

Julie A. Freischlag, MD, FACS, FRCSEd(Hon), DFSVS, MAMSE, is a distinguished vascular surgeon and leader in academic medicine. She currently holds multiple key roles, including chief executive officer and chief academic officer of Atrium Health Wake Forest Baptist, chief academic officer and executive vice president of Advocate Health, and executive vice president for health affairs at Wake Forest University. Throughout her extensive career, Dr. Freischlag has held significant leadership positions such as the dean of Wake Forest University School of Medicine (2018-2023) and vice chancellor for Human Health Sciences and dean of the School of Medicine at UC Davis. She has over 15 years of experience leading education and training programs and more than 30 years leading patient care services as a chief of surgery or vascular surgery at nationally ranked hospitals, including serving as professor, chair of the surgery department, and surgeon-in-chief at Johns Hopkins Medical Institutions. A practicing vascular surgeon, Dr. Freischlag is an internationally recognized expert in treating thoracic outlet syndrome. Her national leadership roles include serving as the 2021-2022 President of the American College of Surgeons. She is an elected member of the National Academy of Medicine (2015) and an inductee of the Academy of Master Surgeon Educators (2021). Dr. Freischlag earned her medical degree from Rush University and completed her surgical residency and vascular surgery fellowship at UCLA Medical Center. She is scheduled to retire at the end of 2025.
