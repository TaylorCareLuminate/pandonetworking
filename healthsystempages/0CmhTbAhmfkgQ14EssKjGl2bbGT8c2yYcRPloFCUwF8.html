Recent News

Jun 16, 2025
A federal lawsuit was filed against Orlando Health earlier this week, specifically on Monday, June 16, 2025. The lawsuit alleges that Orlando Health, a large healthcare network in Florida, failed to provide over $5 million in refunds owed to medical residents. The legal action, brought by attorneys Jordan M. Lewis and T. Scott Tufts, seeks class action status on behalf of former medical residents. According to the lawsuit, Social Security and Medicare contributions were withheld from the residents' pay. Despite rulings that the IRS had to refund contested contributions collected between 1995 and 2005, the lawsuit claims Orlando Health failed to act and advise medical residents about these refund opportunities, only applying for refunds for a single year. The plaintiffs are seeking for Orlando Health to cover or reimburse the lost tax refunds for the affected medical residents.
Jun 06, 2025
Construction has commenced on a new 77,200-square-foot Watson Clinic Medical Office building in Lakeland. This new facility will be situated on the campus of the Orlando Health Lakeland Highlands Hospital, which is currently under construction. The project is being managed by Hoar Construction as the general contractor, with an estimated completion time of mid-2026. The four-story building is designed to house offices and imaging labs, with the first three floors dedicated to offices, exam rooms, and imaging suites equipped with MRI, CT, X-ray, and ultrasound machines. The adjacent Orlando Health Lakeland Highlands Hospital is planned as a 306-bed inpatient facility featuring nearly 30 ICU/progressive care rooms, 16 patient rooms for a women's birthing program, shelled space for a future neonatal intensive care unit, a 48-bed emergency department, and various other medical and support services.
Jun 03, 2025
Orlando Health recently hosted its second annual mental health conference at Orlando Regional Medical Center, where approximately 120 mental health experts convened to discuss the future of mental health, with a particular focus on addressing the loneliness epidemic and the role of technology, including AI. The attendance this year doubled compared to the previous year, bringing together a diverse group of professionals including nurses, doctors, psychiatrists, psychologists, and counselors from the Central Florida area. Experts at the conference highlighted the growing concern of loneliness, citing data from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention indicating that about 29% of Florida residents experience loneliness. Kenny Tello, corporate director of team member well-being at Orlando Health and a conference speaker, noted that while modern communication technologies have brought convenience, they have also contributed to loneliness and disconnection. However, Tello suggested that technology, social media, and AI could be utilized more effectively by mental health professionals to combat issues like loneliness and improve accessibility, combat misinformation, and reduce the stigma associated with seeking mental health services.
May 13, 2025
Orlando Health is collaborating with students and faculty from the University of Central Florida (UCF) to implement an AI system aimed at improving efficiency in robotic surgeries. This project, known as the AIMS (Artificial Intelligence for Medical Surgery) system, was developed by UCF engineering and computer science seniors as part of their Senior Design capstone course. The AI software tracks surgical tools, specifically staples initially, during procedures to provide real-time data to the medical staff. This information helps in managing the quantity of staples needed for a surgery, thereby reducing waste. The system has been tested in an Orlando Health operating room and is currently being used by Dr. Alexis Sanchez, Director of Robotic Surgery at Orlando Health Orlando Regional Medical Center. This partnership highlights the health system's focus on leveraging academic innovation for clinical advancements and efficiency in surgical settings. [3, 5, 12]
May 01, 2025
Orlando Health's Digestive Health Institute has launched an expansion that integrates state-of-the-art AI technology aimed at revolutionizing the early detection of pancreatic cancer. This initiative addresses the critical need for earlier diagnosis of pancreatic cancer, which is a leading cause of cancer deaths in the U.S. The AI-powered endoscopic ultrasound technology, developed in collaboration with European experts, allows clinicians to identify small pancreatic tumors at earlier, more treatable stages. Dr. Shyam Varadarajulu, president of the Institute, emphasizes that artificial intelligence can help doctors find challenging tumors sooner, leading to improved treatment plans. The expansion also supports innovative research, including AI capabilities to improve diagnosis, treatment, and outcomes for diseases like inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). [2, 7, 16]
