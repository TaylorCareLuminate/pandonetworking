Recent News

Jun 11, 2025
Aurora Behavioral Health System in Glendale, AZ, which is a 100-bed free-standing psychiatric inpatient hospital and outpatient program for adolescents and adults, posted a job opening for a Cook on June 11, 2025. The position involves working under the Dietary Director to prepare food according to dietary guidelines for patients, visitors, and employees. The role requires previous food service experience, preferably in a hospital or institutional setting, good communication skills, the ability to work well with others, and the capacity to manage workload effectively. The job posting indicated both full-time and on-call positions were available with an average pay of $17.00 to $20.00 per hour depending on experience. Key responsibilities include planning and organizing food preparation, ensuring proper use of leftovers, maintaining a clean work environment, serving food, storing supplies correctly, monitoring temperatures, and preparing for special functions. Minimum requirements included passing a drug screen and physical, and being eligible for a fingerprint clearance card. The posting also noted that this specific position had been closed.
Mar 13, 2025
Aurora Behavioral Health has reportedly implemented a digital tool from NeuroFlow to support measurement-informed care. This adoption of technology has shown significant positive outcomes, including dramatically faster progress toward patient remission, a reduction in missed appointments for client services, and an increase in capacity as patients improved more quickly. The implementation also led to significant cost reductions for the behavioral health system. This initiative aligns with broader trends in behavioral health innovation, leveraging technology to improve clinical care efficiency and effectiveness.
Jan 29, 2025
A group of 70 to 80 behavioral health operators filed a lawsuit against the Arizona Department of Health Services, the Arizona Health Care Cost Containment System (AHCCCS), and other state officials. The lawsuit alleges "racial discrimination, negligence, and breaches of contract" in connection with the state's Medicaid fraud investigation targeting "sober living" facilities. The plaintiffs claim they were unfairly targeted by AHCCCS when the state suspended payments to facilities in 2023 as part of a crackdown on an estimated $2.5 billion in fraudulent billing. Civil rights attorney Ben Crump is representing the providers and stated that the state agencies have "devastated Native American communities" and the healthcare providers serving them. The providers accuse state investigators of unfairly targeting them in an effort by the state to save face after the scandal. They claim the state's actions harmed facilities primarily serving Native Americans, while seemingly allowing similar facilities serving wealthier communities to continue operating. The lawsuit seeks justice for the providers whose livelihoods were impacted and the communities that lost access to necessary healthcare. Aurora Behavioral Health System accepts AHCCCS plans and operates in Arizona, but this specific news story details a lawsuit brought by a *group* of providers and does not explicitly name Aurora Behavioral Health System as a plaintiff in this particular legal action. Oct 04, 2024
Aurora Behavioral Healthcare System East reported a data privacy incident that disrupted some of their IT systems. The incident was discovered on January 28, 2024, and their investigation determined that an unauthorized party accessed or acquired certain files between January 27 and January 28, 2024. The affected information varied per patient but may have included names, dates of birth, medical record numbers, services received, dates of services, and treating physician. Notification letters were mailed to impacted patients starting October 4, 2024. In response, the system has implemented additional safeguards and technical security measures.
Sep 04, 2024
A digital health news roundup mentioned the collaboration agreement between Alera Health, Affiliated Network Providers (ANP), and Aurora Behavioral Health to provide coordinated care for individuals with behavioral health conditions in Maricopa County, Arizona. The collaboration involves integrating their resources, working on innovative care models, enhancing data sharing, and coordinating whole-person care. Aurora Behavioral Health is noted as having mental health and psychiatric hospitals in Glendale and Tempe, Arizona. This partnership leverages technology to improve the continuum of care between Aurora's inpatient facilities and ANP's outpatient health homes.
