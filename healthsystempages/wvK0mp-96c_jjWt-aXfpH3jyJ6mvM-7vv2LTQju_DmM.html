This achievement highlights CommonSpirit Health's commitment to adopting innovative treatments and advancing cancer care for patients. Jun 11, 2025
CommonSpirit Health's Holy Cross Hospital – Mountain Point in Lehi, Utah, has implemented a new Tele-NICU arrangement in partnership with the University of Utah, providing 24/7 access to neonatologists for babies in the Newborn Intensive Care Unit. This service utilizes a special secure telemedicine unit within the NICU, allowing nurses or pediatricians at Holy Cross Hospital – Mountain Point to instantly connect with a neonatologist at the University of Utah. The technology facilitates real-time monitoring of the baby's vital signs and allows neonatologists to view the baby through a high-resolution camera, enabling the care for sicker and more premature infants locally and potentially avoiding transfers to other facilities. The University of Utah has credentialed 29 board-certified neonatologists for this program, and training has been provided to Holy Cross Hospital – Mountain Point staff on the telemedicine equipment. This is the fourth Utah hospital to benefit from the University of Utah's Tele-NICU program. The system also alerts the flight team in case a transfer to a higher-level NICU is necessary.
Jun 11, 2025
Physicians at CommonSpirit Health's Holy Cross Hospital – Jordan Valley in Salt Lake City, Utah, are the first in the state to use a new radiation therapy called Gamma Tiles for treating brain tumors, including glioblastomas, meningiomas, and brain metastasis. This technology involves placing small, bioabsorbable collagen tiles embedded with radioactive seeds directly into the surgery cavity after a brain tumor is removed. The tiles deliver targeted radiation to the area where the tumor was located to help prevent regrowth while minimizing exposure to healthy brain tissue. The collagen is absorbed over a few months, while the seeds remain in place. This approach allows for immediate radiation treatment, unlike traditional external radiation therapy which often requires a delay. The procedure is a collaboration between neurosurgeons and radiation oncologists. While primarily a medical device and procedure, its implementation represents the adoption of advanced medical technology within the CommonSpirit Health system.
