Jun 16, 2025
Universal Health Services (UHS) has launched an initiative in collaboration with Hippocratic AI, deploying Generative AI healthcare agents to assist clinicians with post-discharge patient engagement. These AI agents are designed to support the monitoring of patients after they leave the hospital, aiming to quickly detect and alert UHS clinical staff to changes in patient conditions and address patient questions. This program is expected to increase patient engagement through more frequent post-discharge phone calls. The initiative was initially rolled out at two UHS subsidiaries: Summerlin Hospital Medical Center in Las Vegas and Texoma Medical Center in Denison, Texas. Thousands of patients have been contacted since the beginning of the year, with the AI agents performing post-discharge check-in calls, reviewing instructions, probing for new or worsening symptoms, and answering questions. The average patient rating for these AI agent interactions has been reported as a favorable 9.0 out of 10. Due to positive results, UHS plans to expand this initiative to other locations. This move reflects UHS's ongoing exploration and experimentation with AI to effectively support the patient care experience. According to AInvest, the launch of this initiative highlights UHS's commitment to leveraging advanced technology to improve patient care and outcomes. [14, 15]

Jun 09, 2025
Universal Health Services's Chief Financial Officer, Steve Filton, commented at a recent healthcare conference that the volume of care patients are seeking has slowed down. Following a surge in voluntary and surgical procedures coming out of the pandemic, volumes have been slower to recover to historical levels than anticipated. While revenue has remained stable due to price increases offsetting the lower volume, the company expects margins in its acute care business to return to pre-pandemic levels over the next 18 to 24 months. Filton also reportedly voiced concern regarding the potential implications of the proposed federal spending bill by the Trump administration on healthcare funding, particularly concerning anticipated cuts to Medicaid, which could significantly impact UHS given its reliance on government programs for a portion of its revenue. These comments led to a decline in Universal Health Services' shares, which fell by nearly 6% following the remarks.
