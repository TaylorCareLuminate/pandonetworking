A study published on June 17, 2025, found 100% patient adherence to a more palatable elemental diet. This diet is used in the treatment of small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO). Traditionally, elemental diets have been described as having an unpleasant, sulfurous taste, leading to poor patient compliance. The improved palatability of this new formulation, studied by a team including Dr. Ali Rezaie, medical director of the GI Motility Program at Cedars-Sinai, and Dr. Mark Pimentel, executive director of Cedars-Sinai's Medically Associated Science and Technology Program, marks a significant advancement in managing conditions that require this type of nutritional intervention, potentially improving treatment outcomes for patients. The View Hospital, in affiliation with Cedars-Sinai, has launched a dedicated dialysis unit in Qatar, marking it as the first private hospital in Qatar to offer such specialized services. This unit aims to enhance comprehensive care and provide world-class kidney care. It offers 24/7 access to hemodialysis care and is equipped with the latest medical technology, including Hemodiafiltration (HDF), an advanced dialysis modality for more effective toxin clearance. The unit features dedicated private rooms for patient comfort and privacy.

Thomas M. Priselac, President & CEO Emeritus of Cedars-Sinai, was honored with the Lifetime Achievement Award at the Partners in Care Foundation's 25th Annual Vision & Excellence in Healthcare Leadership Tribute Dinner. This award has only been granted three times before and recognizes Mr. Priselac's decades of impactful leadership, dedication to healthcare equity, and significant influence on patients, professionals, and communities. Mr. Priselac acknowledged the shared nature of the award with those who have been part of his personal and professional life, including his wife, and highlighted the collaborative efforts within the healthcare community in Los Angeles to improve care despite challenges. He emphasized the collective commitment to making a difference in people's lives through quality, compassion, and innovation, while also recognizing that challenges remain and the need to continue embracing change and prioritizing equitable access to care for all. Cedars-Sinai Medical Center is highlighted for its use of AI in transforming emergency care by revolutionizing ED workforce optimization. The hospital utilizes AI to forecast emergency department patient volumes. This forecasting is achieved by processing years of admission records, considering weather patterns, and even factoring in regional event schedules like concerts that might influence trauma cases. This allows administrators to proactively align staffing, such as nurse-to-patient ratios and specialist availability, with anticipated demand.
