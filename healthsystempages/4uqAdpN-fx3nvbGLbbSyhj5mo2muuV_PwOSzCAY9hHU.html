Jul 01, 2025
Effective July 1, 2025, Advocate physicians group will no longer be part of the Blue Cross Blue Shield HMO Illinois network. However, Cook County's BlueAdvantage HMO plan will continue to be accepted by Advocate physicians group, and this change will not impact members enrolled in the Cook County-sponsored BlueAdvantage HMO. This development signals a shift in network participation for a major physician group within the Blue Cross Blue Shield network in Cook County, which is relevant to the broader landscape of carrier-provider relationships in the area and confirms the continued relationship between Cook County's employee health plan and Advocate physicians despite the change in another Blue Cross Blue Shield network. Jul 01, 2025
A notice on the Cook County website indicates a significant network change affecting employees with Blue Cross Blue Shield HMO Illinois. Effective July 1, 2025, the Advocate physicians group will no longer be accepting the Blue Cross Blue Shield HMO Illinois network. Cook County offers two medical benefit plans: Blue Advantage HMO and Blue Cross Blue Shield PPO. The BlueAdvantage HMO plan, also accepted by Advocate physicians group, will not be affected by this change. This development represents a change in the relationship between a major local physician group and a large health insurance carrier that impacts the network options available to individuals covered by Cook County employee health plans, which could include Cook County Health employees.
Jun 18, 2025
Cook County Health is projecting a budget surplus for 2024 and a balanced budget for 2025, driven in part by increased overall collections from insurers and patients. The health system's Chief Financial Officer, Pamela Cassara, noted an improvement in the collection rate from insurers and patients, rising from 32% to 38%. This improvement is attributed to clinical staff focusing on obtaining prior authorization from insurers and enhancing documentation specificity to ensure more services rendered are billed for and reimbursed. Cook County Health has also maintained a high rate of retaining Medicaid recipients amid federal redetermination processes, contributing to higher-than-anticipated CountyCare membership and patient volume. The projected surplus for fiscal year 2024, ending November 30, is $224.6 million, contributing to the county's overall projected surplus. This positive financial outlook is linked to improved revenue cycle management and successful interactions with payers. [4, 16]
Jun 16, 2025
The Cook County Department of Public Health has shared a warning from the CDC regarding a Salmonella outbreak linked to Emek-brand pistachio cream. Consumers, retailers, restaurants, and distributors are advised not to eat, sell, or serve Emek-brand pistachio cream with a specific use-by date of October 19, 2026, and production code PNO: 241019. As of June 13, 2025, four cases of Salmonella Oranienburg infection had been reported from two states, Minnesota and New Jersey, with one hospitalization. Investigations by the CDC, FDA, and state and local public health officials are ongoing to identify if additional lots or products are affected and to trace the distribution of the contaminated product. While the reported cases are not currently in Illinois, the Cook County Department of Public Health is disseminating this information as a public health alert to make residents and food establishments aware of the potential risk.
Jun 16, 2025
The Chicago Transit Authority (CTA) Board has approved an intergovernmental agreement with Cook County Health (CCH) to install vending machines dispensing free naloxone at five CTA rail stations. These locations were strategically chosen based on geographic analysis that highlighted areas with high opioid-related emergency medical responses and community input. This initiative is part of Cook County Health's regional behavioral health strategic plan, utilizing technology for dispensing life-saving medication and analytics for targeted placement to combat overdose deaths.
