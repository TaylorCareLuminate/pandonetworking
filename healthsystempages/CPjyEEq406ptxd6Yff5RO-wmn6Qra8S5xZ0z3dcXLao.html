Recent News

Jun 16, 2025
Researchers from Mass General Brigham and the United States Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) have developed a deep learning algorithm capable of detecting coronary artery calcium levels in chest CT scans. This AI tool, named AI-CAC, can analyze existing CT scans to identify individuals at higher risk for cardiovascular events and premature death. Published in NEJM AI, the research indicates high accuracy and predictive value for future heart attacks and 10-year mortality. According to Hugo Aerts, PhD, director of the Artificial Intelligence in Medicine (AIM) Program at Mass General Brigham, millions of chest CT scans are performed annually, often for other reasons, and this tool can leverage that data to proactively assess cardiovascular risk. The findings suggest that widespread implementation of AI-CAC could help clinicians identify patients who might benefit from earlier intervention, such as lipid-lowering therapy. While the study was conducted on a veteran population, the team aims to perform future studies on the general population. Mass General Brigham authors on the paper include Simon Bernatz and Leonard Nürnberg.
Jun 10, 2025
Primary care physicians at Mass General Brigham are continuing their efforts to unionize following a vote last month where they overwhelmingly supported forming a union by a margin of 183-26. This marks the first instance of academic primary care physicians in Massachusetts taking such a step. However, the certification of the union is currently in limbo as Mass General Brigham has appealed to the National Labor Relations Board (NLRB) to review the vote and dispute which physicians are eligible to be part of the bargaining unit. The healthcare system argues that the proposed unit, which includes physicians at acute care hospitals without encompassing all physicians in those hospitals, is contrary to established NLRB rules. Despite the legal hurdles and potential delays due to the lack of a quorum at the NLRB in Washington, D.C., the physicians maintain that unionization is necessary to improve patient care, address staff shortages, and combat burnout from long hours. Jun 05, 2025
A study conducted by Mass General Brigham researchers evaluated the effectiveness of integrating large language models (LLMs) such as OpenAI's GPT-4 and Google's Gemini 1.5 with traditional diagnostic decision support systems (DDSS) for improving patient diagnoses. The study compared the performance of these LLMs to Mass General Brigham's established diagnostic tool, DXplain, which utilizes a comprehensive database to generate potential diagnoses. DXplain demonstrated superior accuracy with a 72% rate compared to 64% for ChatGPT and 58% for Gemini. While DXplain was found to be more effective in providing differential diagnoses, particularly when laboratory data was involved, the findings suggest that incorporating LLMs could still enhance clinical decision-making by offering additional insights. The study indicates a potential synergy between AI models and traditional diagnostic tools.

Mass General Brigham Health Plan has received significant industry recognition for its Medicare Advantage campaign titled "Live in your moment." Executive Leadership

Anne Klibanski, MD

President and Chief Executive Officer

Anne Klibanski, MD, is the President and Chief Executive Officer at Mass General Brigham. She earned her B.A., magna cum laude in literature from Barnard College and her M.D. from New York University Medical Center. Dr. Klibanski is the Laurie Carrol Guthart Professor of Medicine at Harvard Medical School. Prior to her current role, she served as the healthcare system's Chief Academic Officer from 2012 to 2019 and as Chief of Neuroendocrine at Massachusetts General Hospital. She is internationally recognized for her research in neuroendocrine disorders and pituitary tumors. She was appointed President and CEO in June 2019, becoming the first woman to hold this position at Mass General Brigham (formerly Partners HealthCare).

Margaret S. Norton

Chief of Staff

Margaret S. Norton is the Chief of Staff at Mass General Brigham, a role she has held since 2017. In this capacity, she is responsible for coordinating activities related to the CEO to support Mass General Brigham's objectives, including providing research, strategic counsel, and editorial support. She also collaborates closely with Mass General Brigham leadership and external stakeholders. Before her role at Mass General Brigham, Norton was the Director of Corporate Communications at Eversource (previously NSTAR) for nearly 20 years. Norton earned a Bachelor of Arts in English Literature and Political Science from Emmanuel College and a Master of Science in Communications Management from Simmons College. Paul Anderson, MD, PhD

Chief Academic Officer

Paul Anderson, MD, PhD, serves as the Chief Academic Officer at Mass General Brigham. He is also the K. Frank Austen Professor of Medicine at Harvard Medical School and the Academic Dean for Mass General Brigham at Harvard Medical School. Dr. Anderson earned his MD and PhD in Microbiology from the New York University School of Medicine. He completed his internship and residency in Internal Medicine and a fellowship in Rheumatology at Brigham and Women's Hospital. Dr. Anderson established his research laboratory at Brigham and Women's Hospital in 1990, focusing on the post-transcriptional control of gene expression during stress and inflammatory responses. He previously served as the Associate Chief of the Division of Rheumatology, Inflammation, and Immunity and was appointed Chief Academic Officer at Brigham and Women's Hospital in 2015. He assumed the role of Chief Academic Officer for Mass General Brigham on an interim basis in January 2023 and was permanently appointed in October 2023. In his current role, he oversees Mass General Brigham's research and teaching enterprise, which includes managing a significant research budget and key system-wide departments. He has an internationally recognized reputation for his research and has authored or co-authored over 175 publications.
