Jun 12, 2025
Veterans and advocates rallied at the Northport VA Medical Center on June 12, 2025, to protest potential nationwide workforce reductions proposed by the current administration. The planned cuts could reportedly amount to a 15% reduction in the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs workforce. Those protesting, including veterans who receive care at the facility and union officials, expressed concerns that these reductions could negatively impact the quality of patient care and overburden existing staff at the Northport facility. They fear that the proposed restructuring of the federal agency could severely affect the Long Island facility, which serves thousands of veterans in the region. Union officials highlighted existing staff shortages at the Northport VA and argued that further cuts would exacerbate these issues, potentially leading to employee burnout and diminished services for veterans. Senator Kirsten Gillibrand also voiced concerns regarding the potential impact of VA funding cutbacks on resources for veterans statewide, including the possibility of jeopardizing benefits under the PACT Act. VA officials, however, maintain that the planned changes are aimed at redirecting funds from non-mission-critical areas and will not negatively impact veteran health care or benefits. [4, 5]
May 20, 2025
The Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) is exploring the use of artificial intelligence (AI) to enhance its software license management, aiming to identify underutilized, redundant, or renegotiable licenses for cost savings. This effort is part of the VA's Enterprise Software Assessment Management program, established in January 2024, which is working to gain comprehensive visibility into software usage across the agency. Jeff VanBemmel, executive director of end user operations within VA's Office of Information and Technology, testified that while AI for software asset management is a relatively new investment expected to be fully fielded later in 2025, it presents an opportunity to analyze large datasets and understand software usage more efficiently. The program has already achieved significant cost avoidance through manual reforms and new tools for managing licenses for the top 15 commercial software programs. By leveraging AI, the VA intends to streamline the process of identifying discrepancies between purchased and utilized software licenses and address instances where multiple software titles serve the same function, with the goal of consolidating options for better value. This initiative, part of the VA's broader IT strategy, will impact software management practices at all VA facilities, including Northport. [11]
May 16, 2025
The Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) Program Contracting Activity Central (PCAC) is seeking sources for a firm-fixed-price contract for Architect-Engineer (A-E) services related to the design review, pre-bid revisions, solicitation support, construction period services, and commissioning for Project #632-22-700, EHRM Infrastructure Upgrades at the Northport, NY VA Medical Center. This project is set aside entirely for Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business (SDVOSB) concerns. The estimated construction cost for these Electronic Health Records Modernization (EHRM) infrastructure upgrades is $50 million. The anticipated A-E contract is expected to be awarded by August 2025. The A-E services required during the construction period include reviewing construction submittals, supporting requests for information, assisting with construction contract changes, generating meeting minutes, and providing punch-list reports. The solicitation emphasizes the need for experience in addressing unforeseen conditions and emergent situations during construction. This project signifies a significant investment in the core IT infrastructure at Northport to support the VA's ongoing EHR modernization efforts.
Apr 08, 2025
A bipartisan bill, the Vietnam Veterans Liver Fluke Cancer Study Act, introduced by Rep. Nick LaLota (R-Suffolk County) and co-led by Rep. Pat Ryan (D-NY), passed the House of Representatives. This legislation builds upon a 2018 study conducted at the Northport Veterans Affairs Medical Center which examined liver fluke infection among Vietnam Veterans. The Act mandates the Department of Veterans Affairs, in collaboration with the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), to undertake a comprehensive study to determine the prevalence of bile duct cancer among Vietnam War veterans and investigate a potential connection to liver fluke exposure during their service in Southeast Asia. Rep. LaLota emphasized the urgent need for this larger-scale investigation to provide answers and ensure veterans receive appropriate care and recognition for this condition, stating that the House's action addresses the VA's previous inaction on the issue. Mar 21, 2025
The Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) Chief AI Officer (CAIO), Charles Worthington, discussed the agency's plans to expand the use of artificial intelligence across the department in 2025. Speaking with MeriTalk, Worthington, who is also the VA's chief technology officer, noted a significant increase in the number of AI use cases within the VA from 2023 to 2024 and projected continued growth. The primary goal for 2025 is to implement useful AI products throughout the VA by focusing on the department's most critical challenges and identifying how AI can provide solutions. One successful use case mentioned is an on-network generative AI chat interface for VA employees, similar to commercial tools but designed for secure use with VA data. A pilot program for this tool has shown promising early results, with a high percentage of users reporting increased efficiency. Another AI tool highlighted assists in colonoscopies, demonstrating a statistically significant increase in adenoma detection rates in a recent VA study. The VA is also piloting an 'ambient scribe' AI tool to aid in clinical encounters by summarizing information for electronic health records, aiming to save clinicians time. While this article discusses VA-wide initiatives, these advancements indicate the technological direction of the VA, which could see implementation or pilot programs extend to facilities like the Northport VA.
