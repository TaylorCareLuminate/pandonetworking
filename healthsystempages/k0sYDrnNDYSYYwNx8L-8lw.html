Jun 12, 2025
Texas public health departments are preparing for an additional $119 million in federal funding cuts expected at the end of June. These cuts could significantly affect the state's efforts in disease detection and prevention, including programs for HIV, diabetes, vaccine outreach, and emergency preparedness. This comes after the federal government previously reclaimed $700 million in unspent COVID-19 pandemic funds. The timing is challenging as the state legislative session recently ended, preventing the Texas Department of State Health Services (DSHS) from requesting more state funds to offset these losses. While state funding to DSHS increased by $86 million for the next two years, the agency anticipates losing a total of $685 million in federal funds, largely due to the end of COVID-19 funding. The chief deputy commissioner of DSHS, Imelda Garcia, mentioned this potential $119 million cut at a committee meeting, noting that they are still awaiting confirmation and details from federal partners. These impending cuts from Centers for Disease Control and Prevention grants could impact various critical areas, including $28 million for vaccine coverage, $36.4 million for preparing communities for public health threats, $20 million for hospital preparedness, and $25.5 million for HIV prevention. Jun 10, 2025
The Texas Department of State Health Services (DSHS) reported an update on the measles outbreak in the South Plains region of Texas on June 10, 2025. DSHS considers this an ongoing outbreak, and due to the highly contagious nature of measles, they anticipate additional cases in the outbreak area and surrounding regions. As part of their response, DSHS is updating the list of counties included in the designated outbreak area. The listed counties now include Cochran, Dallam, Dawson, Gaines, Garza, Lynn, Lamar, Lubbock, Terry, and Yoakum. DSHS is working with local health departments in the affected area to investigate cases and provide assistance. [6, 10]
Jun 10, 2025
The Texas Department of State Health Services (DSHS) has brought together multiple schools of public health under a unified program called the Academic Health Partnership Initiative. This initiative, led by the DSHS Office of Practice and Learning within the Center for Public Health Policy and Practice, aims to strengthen, support, and enhance collaborations between public health practice and academic institutions across Texas's vast and decentralized public health system. Recognizing the importance of partnerships in a state with 254 counties where local health departments operate independently and the state provides services where none exist, DSHS believes these Academic Health Department (AHD) partnerships foster accountability, clearer collective value, and improved access to funding opportunities. These collaborations can encompass various activities, from student internships to fully integrated partnerships and shared resources, ultimately working to enhance public health workforce development and build a more resilient and connected public health infrastructure in Texas. DSHS highlights that these partnerships improve the relevance of education to public health practice, foster innovative practices and research, strengthen connections and trust, facilitate the sharing of evidence-based projects, maximize resources and expertise, and help achieve strategic goals.
Jun 06, 2025
The Texas Department of State Health Services (DSHS) reported no new measles cases on June 6, 2025, for the first time since the outbreak began in late January. This update brings the total number of confirmed cases in the outbreak, primarily located in West Texas, to 742. Fewer than 10 of these confirmed cases are estimated to be actively infectious. Since the start of the outbreak, 94 individuals have been hospitalized, and there have been two fatalities in unvaccinated school-aged children with no known underlying conditions. DSHS has designated Cochran, Dawson, Gaines, Lamar, Lubbock, Terry, and Yoakum counties as active outbreak counties with ongoing measles transmission. The agency announced that it will transition from providing twice-weekly measles updates to weekly updates, which will be released every Tuesday, starting June 10, as the outbreak-related cases continue to slow.
